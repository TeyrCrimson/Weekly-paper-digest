# Weekly summary

## The week in one paragraph
This week's eight digests cluster around three threads: calibrating monocular/panoramic depth and scene-completion models, squeezing efficient active perception out of UAV camera and sensor control, and testing remote-sensing robustness without access to target-domain data. The two strongest entries (both 8/10) offer genuinely new mechanisms — treating a depth model's output coordinate as a per-image free parameter, and substituting camera pan-tilt-zoom for flight-path changes to resolve small/distant targets — and back them with evidence that holds up across multiple benchmarks or simulated conditions. Nearly everything else, however, is simulation-only, single-seed, or evaluated on a hand-curated academic benchmark, so "state-of-the-art" claims here should be read as benchmark-conditional, not field-ready. Several papers (the UAV search planner, the multi-UAV state estimator, the video-diffusion navigation paper) are explicit that their headline advantage is confined to a specific regime — high altitude, aggressive maneuvers, truncated inference — and ties or loses to simpler baselines outside it. No paper reports quantitative real-world robotic deployment; where hardware is mentioned, it's qualitative demonstration only.

## Themes
- **Recalibrating monocular/panoramic depth and scene completion** — *Boosting Metric Depth Completion via Training-Free Adaptive Response Geometry*, *Decoupling Spherical Reasoning from Dense Prediction for 360 Depth Estimation*, and *CrowdOcc* all attack the same underlying problem: a frozen depth/vision foundation model's raw output needs structure-aware correction (coordinate choice, spherical distortion, occlusion-aware fusion) rather than a single global affine fix. Collectively they establish that this correction is worth doing — the training-free depth method beats trained baselines, and CrowdOcc beats generic SSC baselines — but gains are often modest (CrowdOcc: +1–2 IoU points) or come with trade-offs (the depth paper improves metric accuracy while *worsening* surface-normal accuracy).
- **Active/efficient perception for UAV autonomy under hard constraints** — *Online Planning for Sparse Ground Target Search* (camera PTZ instead of repositioning), *Towards Agile Vision-Based Multi-UAV Flight* (tilt instead of position-derivative lag), and the video-diffusion navigation paper (truncated denoising instead of full generation) share a pattern: exploit an underused signal to beat a brute-force baseline specifically in a hard regime. All three show real gains in that regime (high altitude, >2g maneuvers, low-latency replanning) but admit baselines are competitive or better outside it, and none has been validated in real closed-loop flight with quantitative results.
- **Remote-sensing perception without target-domain data** — *Raw Imagery Impacting Your AI* (does ship detection need "clean" restored imagery?) and *COBICount* (counting unseen object categories with zero target data) both probe whether perception models can tolerate degraded or novel conditions without retraining. Together they show the answer is "sometimes, unevenly" — degradation/domain-shift sensitivity is real but architecture- and condition-dependent, and both papers' statistical backing is thin (single-seed training runs, small degradation grids, no variance analysis).
- **A cross-cutting evidentiary caveat** — simulation-only evaluation (UAV search, multi-UAV state estimation closed-loop test, navigation paper's quantitative benchmark) and small/single-run samples (Raw Imagery, COBICount, CrowdOcc) recur so often this week that the field-readiness of almost every headline number should be treated as provisional.

## Read these
- **Boosting Metric Depth Completion via Training-Free Adaptive Response Geometry** — the cleanest idea of the week (output coordinate as a fitted parameter, not a hand-picked choice), beats three *trained* baselines with no training at all, and the authors are unusually honest about where it trades off (normal error) and doesn't dominate (4/9 subsets).
- **Online Planning for Sparse Ground Target Search from a High-Altitude UAV** — the most novel framing (camera control instead of motion planning to respect airspace rules) with the broadest simulated evidence (multiple altitudes, towns, baselines) and a refreshingly direct admission of exactly where it doesn't win (low altitude).
- **Raw Imagery Impacting Your AI: Should You Care?** — unlike the other remote-sensing entry, this one answers a concrete system-design question (can onboard AI skip image restoration?) with a real, if narrow, interaction finding (combined blur+noise is worse than either alone) that's directly actionable for sensor/mission designers.

## Skip unless
- **CrowdOcc** — you need monocular scene completion for a legged robot in crowded indoor spaces and want a real (if small, 11-scene) RGB-D dataset with human-occupancy labels to build on.
- **COBICount** — you must count objects in satellite imagery for a category/domain you have zero labeled data for, and academic-benchmark-level validation (not real deployment) is sufficient.
- **Decoupling Spherical Reasoning from Dense Prediction for 360 Depth Estimation** — you're already building a panoramic (ERP-based) depth pipeline and want distortion-aware context reasoning added without redesigning the backbone.
- **The video-diffusion UAV navigation paper (DiffWAM/FastDreamer)** — you're already using a large video world model for drone trajectory planning and need to cut onboard latency by reading motion out of truncated denoising passes instead of full generation.
- **Towards Agile Vision-Based Multi-UAV Flight: Revisiting State Estimation** — your pipeline already extracts a target drone's 6D pose (not just position) and you need lower-latency velocity/acceleration estimates for aggressive (>1g) multi-UAV tracking.
