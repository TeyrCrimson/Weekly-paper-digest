# FOCUS & RePAIR: Mitigating Text Degeneration via Token-Level Guidance for Pruned Large Language Models

**Authors:** Junyoung Lee, Sehyeon Park, Shinhyoung Jang, Seonha Ryu, Hojeong Kim, Hyunsei Lee, Il Hong Suh, Yeseong Kim

**arXiv:** [2608.26676](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26676)

**Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG

**Submitted:** 2026-08-27

**Relevance:** 10/10 — matched: Post-inference detection and repair of degenerate output

---

## TL;DR
Pruning LLMs (via LoRA fine-tuning + distillation afterward) makes repetition loops much worse even though perplexity and accuracy look fine, and the paper traces this to two token-level failure modes: tokens that shouldn't be sampled leaking probability mass ("loop entry"), and too little probability left on plausible alternatives once in a repetitive context ("loop persistence"). They propose two training-time fixes — FOCUS (a reweighted distillation loss that suppresses leakage into teacher-suppressed tokens) and RePAIR (a margin loss trained on onset-centered positive/negative continuation pairs that boosts escape-token probability) — which together cut repetition substantially with only a small perplexity cost.

## First-principles breakdown

**The underlying problem.** Compressing an LLM via pruning (removing attention heads/MLP channels — "width pruning," or entire transformer blocks — "depth pruning") is a standard way to cut inference cost, but it destroys some learned behavior. The standard fix is to fine-tune the pruned model afterward (often with LoRA, a cheap low-rank adapter, plus knowledge distillation from the original unpruned "teacher" model) on a small instruction dataset like Alpaca. This is usually judged successful if perplexity and zero-shot task accuracy come back close to baseline. The paper's starting observation (Table 1) is that this metric is misleading: even after such fine-tuning, the pruned model repeats itself far more often than the original — e.g., greedy decoding repetition jumps from 26.6% to over 63%. Standard metrics don't catch this because a model can assign a reasonable overall probability distribution and still get trapped in a self-reinforcing repetition loop at generation time.

**Why prior fixes fall short.** Existing anti-repetition tricks either operate at decode time (top-k/top-p sampling, which just restricts the candidate token pool) or modify the training loss by penalizing tokens that already appeared (unlikelihood training, ScaleGrad) or building synthetic repeated sentences to penalize (DITTO). These indiscriminately push probability away from repeated tokens without telling the model what a *good* alternative continuation is, so they tend to hurt perplexity and produce incoherent output while only partially fixing repetition.

**Reframing repetition as a dynamical system.** The paper's core conceptual move: treat text generation as a stochastic process moving between "contexts" (recent token windows). Repetition-prone contexts form a small region L that the process can enter and get stuck in. They define:
- **Coverage / CREP**: a way to measure repetition — the fraction of a generated sequence explained by its single most-repeated n-gram; a sequence is "degenerate" if this exceeds a threshold. Averaged over many samples, this becomes the CREP rate.
- **Loop entry risk (R_T)**: probability the trajectory ever enters the repetitive region L within T steps.
- **Loop persistence (ρ(c))**: once inside L, the probability of staying inside L at the next step, from context c.
They show mathematically (Eq. 6) that overall degeneration probability is bounded by entry risk times persistence raised to a power (the number of consecutive in-loop steps needed to trigger detection). Since persistence is exponentiated, even small reductions in persistence yield outsized reductions in long repetition runs — this is the paper's key mechanistic insight, and they support it empirically by showing that fixing just the first two tokens after a repetition "onset" collapses CREP dramatically (Table 2).

**What controls persistence: escape mass.** Under nucleus (top-p) sampling, the model only samples from the smallest token set S_p(c) covering probability mass p. Within that set, some tokens continue the loop (mass a(c)) and some let the model escape it (mass e(c)); persistence ρ(c) = a(c)/(a(c)+e(c)). So to reduce persistence, training must increase the probability mass on plausible non-repetitive alternatives *inside the nucleus set*, not just spread out the full vocabulary distribution (which wouldn't affect nucleus sampling much).

**Why pruning + naive distillation makes this worse.** Standard distillation minimizes forward KL between teacher and pruned student. Forward KL, under a capacity-constrained student that can't represent all teacher modes, tends to "mode-average" — spreading probability mass in ways that (a) leak some mass onto tokens the teacher considered implausible (increasing entry risk into loops) and (b) collapse near-tied plausible alternatives into one dominant token (reducing escape mass, i.e., increasing persistence). Both effects push toward more repetition even while aggregate metrics like perplexity stay flat.

**The two proposed fixes.**
- **FOCUS**: modifies the distillation loss by multiplying each token's KL contribution by a weight w(q) that emphasizes tokens where the teacher is confident (high q) — a function of teacher probability with tunable exponents β, γ. The gradient analysis shows this is mathematically equivalent to distilling against a *reweighted* teacher distribution that suppresses low-confidence (leakage-prone) probability mass, directly targeting entry risk.
- **RePAIR**: since detecting a loop's onset requires seeing future tokens, this can't be done at inference time, so instead they build training pairs: run the pruned model, detect where a repetition loop begins (via Coverage), take that prefix, and get (a) a negative continuation (the pruned model's actual repetitive output) and (b) a positive continuation (regenerated by the *unpruned* model from the same prefix). A margin-ranking loss then trains the student to assign lower loss (higher likelihood) to the positive continuation than the negative one, specifically over the tokens right after the onset — directly boosting escape-mass at loop-sensitive contexts.
Both losses are combined additively with the normal cross-entropy loss, with small fixed weights.

## Key insights

- **Genuinely new**: the entry/persistence decomposition of repetition as a controllable dynamical quantity, and the identification that within-nucleus "escape mass" (not entropy over the full vocabulary) is the lever that matters — this is a useful reframing beyond prior heuristic anti-repetition losses, and it's exercised empirically (onset-token-replacement experiment, Table 2, and the persistence-tracking experiment, Figure 4) rather than purely theoretical.
- **Supported by evidence**: FOCUS and RePAIR both reduce CREP/n-gram repetition and improve MAUVE/BERTScore/EAD1 across two model families (Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-2-13B), two task types (open-ended continuation, instruction generation), multiple pruning methods (LLMPruner, Shortened-LLaMA, FLAP) and pruning ratios (25/35/45%), and even on a different model family (Qwen 2.5-3B) — this breadth is a real strength supporting generality claims.
- **RePAIR outperforms DPO** in a matched comparison (Table 5), supporting the claim that onset-level (token-local) supervision beats sequence-level preference learning for this specific failure mode — a useful, testable claim, not just asserted.
- **Combining FOCUS+RePAIR gives the best results** in almost all tables, and FOCUS is shown to also help other existing mitigation baselines (UL, SG, DITTO) when stacked with them — supports the "compatible/composable" claim, though this composability is somewhat expected given FOCUS operates on the distillation loss while the others modify decoding-adjacent objectives.
- The claimed distinctiveness vs. ToDi (another distribution-shaping distillation method) is supported by one head-to-head table showing FOCUS achieves much lower repetition, though ToDi's MAUVE is comparable — so the comparison is somewhat metric-dependent (see caveats).

## Caveats — stated and inferred

- **Perplexity consistently increases** with FOCUS/RePAIR (stated) — the authors acknowledge and try to address this by showing TruthfulQA doesn't degrade, but this is only one downstream benchmark used to argue "robustness to likelihood instability"; it doesn't establish that PPL increase is harmless in general (inferred).
- **All experiments use LLMPruner/LoRA/Alpaca as the primary pipeline**, with other pruning methods (Shortened-LLaMA, FLAP) and ratios only in the appendix as secondary checks (inferred from structure — main claims lean on one pruning/fine-tuning recipe).
- **Repetition threshold and hyperparameters are hand-tuned** (e.g., CREP threshold set to 0.3, β/γ/α1/α2 chosen via grid search reported in Appendix F) — the sensitivity of results to these choices outside the tested ranges is not discussed (inferred).
- **RePAIR's positive samples come from the unpruned "teacher" model** — meaning RePAIR requires running the full unpruned model at training-data-generation time, which is not a trivial engineering cost the abstract/main text foregrounds; also positive data may itself reflect whatever behaviors the unpruned model has including its own biases (inferred).
- **LLM-as-a-judge evaluation uses only one judge model** (GPT-120B reasoning) with a fairly simple win/tie/lose protocol and swap-order tie-breaking; no inter-judge agreement or human evaluation is reported (stated setup, but lack of cross-validation is inferred as a limitation).
- **No comparison against decoding-time mitigations** (top-k/top-p variants, contrastive/repetition-penalty decoding, or beam-search alternatives) as baselines in the main tables — comparisons are restricted to training-time baselines (KD, UL, SG, DITTO, and later DPO/ToDi) (inferred, since decoding methods are discussed in related work but not benchmarked head-to-head).
- **The theoretical bound (Eq. 6)** is a one-directional upper bound (P(Coverage≥τ) ≤ R_T · ρ̄^{ℓ_τ}), and ρ̄ (the sup over persistence) is never directly estimated — the paper substitutes an "empirical approximation" via mean probability of repeated tokens (explicitly stated as an approximation, not a direct measurement, in a footnote and Appendix A) (stated).
- **Data construction for RePAIR could leak information**: negative/positive pairs are constructed from the same prefixes the model is later evaluated on style (WikiText-103 continuations), raising a question about how distinct the training and evaluation distributions really are — not addressed in the paper.
- **No wall-clock/compute cost comparison** between FOCUS/RePAIR and baselines (e.g., extra cost of generating pairwise data with the unpruned model, or of running distillation) — not addressed in the paper.
- **Societal impact statement is boilerplate** ("no consequences we feel must be highlighted") despite the paper being about making compressed LLMs behave more like their larger counterparts, which could have downstream implications for deployed systems — inferred as a superficial treatment rather than a real limitation of the technical work.
