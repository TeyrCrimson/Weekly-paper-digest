# Weekly summary

## The week in one paragraph
This was a depth- and geometry-heavy week: three papers (UniMetric, MTDv2, and a self-supervised depth scalability paper) attack different facets of monocular depth's oldest problem — no absolute scale and poor cross-dataset generalization — with training-free calibration, hybrid explicit+learned propagation, and structural-bias decoupling, respectively. Two large surveys catalogued the infrared small-target-detection literature (traditional/model-based in Part 1, deep-learning in Part 2) without adding new experiments of their own. Two papers pushed efficient, hardware-validated perception into the real world: a neuromorphic MAV navigation stack running partly on an actual Loihi chip, and a pruning+quantization pipeline for an embedded traffic-sign reader verified bit-exact on a Jetson. A smartphone-based utility-inspection system rounded out the week, notable mainly for explicitly refusing to claim a reliability benefit it didn't measure. Across the board, the strongest evidence this week is hardware-measured (Loihi power draw, bit-exact Jetson verification) rather than purely simulated or self-reported.

## Themes
- **Fixing depth foundation models' scale and generalization gaps**: *UniMetric*, *The Midas Touch for Metric Depth (MTDv2)*, and *Learning to Discriminate Scene Structures (MaSS)* collectively establish that today's relative-depth/geometry foundation models carry two distinct, addressable defects — arbitrary scale and dataset-specific shortcut learning — and that multiple independent fixes (VLM-prior calibration, geodesic propagation + learned residuals, structural/contextual pathway separation) all show real gains on broad benchmarks. None closes the gap fully: UniMetric's own oracle comparison shows a persistent gap, MTDv2 admits real latency cost and failure propagation from a bad backbone, and MaSS's evidence is truncated in the digest.
- **Infrared small-target detection as a fragmented, fast-growing field**: *Part 1* (traditional/low-rank methods) and *Part 2* (deep learning methods) together survey ~1050+ papers and conclude no method handles all real-world challenges, citing duplicate acronyms, inconsistent benchmarks, and an "evaluation gap" where metrics never measure a priori scene difficulty. These are pure literature aggregation — every quantitative claim is secondhand from the original papers, not re-verified.
- **Hardware-validated efficiency for embedded perception**: *Towards bio-inspired visual navigation* (event camera + SNN on a Loihi chip) and the untitled channel-replay-pruning/quantization paper for an embedded traffic-sign reader (bit-exact INT8 pipeline on Jetson) both demonstrate genuine, hardware-measured power/latency reductions rather than simulated estimates alone. Both are also narrow in scope: the MAV system only partially runs on true neuromorphic silicon and shows a real sim-to-real success-rate drop, while the sign reader is validated only on Korean signage with poor close-up/large-sign robustness.
- **Field deployment that's honest about what it didn't prove**: the *Smartphone-Based Power Distribution Inspection* system is the clearest example this week of a deployed pipeline whose authors explicitly state the data do not support a reliability-improvement claim, despite flagging over a thousand defects — a useful contrast to papers that let hardware/deployment framing imply more impact than measured.

## Read these
- **Towards bio-inspired visual navigation**: the only paper this week with an actual physical system on real neuromorphic silicon (Loihi) and real flights, not just simulation or benchmark tables — rarer and harder evidence than its neighbors.
- **UniMetric**: broadest and most rigorously tested fix for depth-scale ambiguity (9 models, 12 benchmark subsets, consistent 54–84% error reduction), with its own oracle comparison honestly showing the remaining gap — pick this over MTDv2 if you only have time for one scale-recovery paper.
- **The Midas Touch for Metric Depth (MTDv2)**: complements UniMetric by targeting the sparse-observation calibration setting (LiDAR/RGB-D/stereo) rather than VLM priors, with the most extensive cross-dataset testing (16 datasets) and an unusually transparent accounting of where it still fails (DDAD RMSE regression, CPU-only bottleneck).

## Skip unless
- **Learning to Discriminate Scene Structures (MaSS)**: skip unless you're specifically building self-supervised depth models on mixed/diverse training data — the digest is truncated, so its full evidence can't be weighed here.
- **IRSTD survey Part 1 (traditional/low-rank methods)**: skip unless you need a historical literature map of pre-deep-learning infrared target detection — no new experiments.
- **IRSTD survey Part 2 (deep learning methods)**: skip unless you need the companion literature map for modern IRSTD architectures — same caveat, purely a taxonomy of others' reported results.
- **The channel-replay pruning/quantization paper for the embedded sign reader**: skip unless you're deploying detection models on integer-only, memory-constrained hardware — results are Korea-only and several pruning-method comparisons aren't statistically significant end-to-end.
- **Smartphone-Based Power Distribution Inspection**: skip unless you're designing a field-inspection AI workflow for utility infrastructure — the authors themselves say the data don't support any reliability-improvement claim, so treat this as a deployment-workflow reference, not an efficacy result.
