## TL;DR
This is Part 2 of a two-part survey on infrared small target detection (IRSTD), cataloging the explosion of deep-learning approaches (CNNs, Transformers, Mamba, GANs, diffusion, Mixture-of-Experts, KANs, SAM-adapters, LLM/VLM-based, and graph/hypergraph methods) that have overtaken the traditional model-based and low-rank/sparse-decomposition methods covered in Part 1. It organizes hundreds of papers by architecture, loss function, attention/guidance mechanism, and learning paradigm (point-supervised, weakly-supervised, domain-adaptive), and reports which methods claim state-of-the-art on which benchmarks. It is a reference/taxonomy document, not a paper proposing a new method or running new experiments.

## First-principles breakdown

**The underlying problem.** Infrared small target detection means finding a target that occupies only a handful of pixels (a few to a few dozen) in a thermal image, against cluttered, noisy backgrounds, often with low contrast and no clear texture or shape (a "blob" rather than a recognizable object). This is used in military/surveillance contexts (e.g., spotting a distant drone or missile). Classic computer-vision detectors (YOLO, DETR, SAM) are built for everyday objects with rich texture and are not designed for sub-pixel-scale, low-SNR thermal blobs, so they transfer poorly.

**Why generic deep learning architectures struggle, and the fixes proposed:**
- *CNNs*: Convolutional networks build up features via local receptive fields and repeated downsampling. For small targets, repeated downsampling itself destroys the signal — a target that's only a few pixels wide can simply vanish by the time the network reaches deep layers. CNNs also can't model long-range context (e.g., "is this blob consistent with background clutter elsewhere in the image?") because their receptive field is local. Fix attempts: specialized convolutions (Pinwheel, Chinese-Knot), or hybridizing with attention/Transformers.
- *Transformers*: Self-attention computes a weighted average over all positions — mathematically this acts like a low-pass filter, smoothing the image. But a small infrared target is essentially a sharp, high-frequency spike, so the very mechanism that gives Transformers their global context tends to smear out the target as "noise." They're also quadratically expensive in image resolution, which is costly for high-res infrared frames. Swin Transformers (windowed attention) are presented as a partial fix since they localize attention computation.
- *Mamba (state-space models)*: An alternative to attention that processes sequences with linear-time recurrence instead of quadratic attention, via techniques like 2D-Selective-Scan (SS2D) that unfold the 2D image into scan directions. Pitched as cheaper than Transformers while still capturing long-range dependency.
- *KANs (Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks)*: Replace fixed activation functions with learnable nonlinear functions on edges, based on the Kolmogorov-Arnold representation theorem (any multivariate continuous function can be expressed as compositions/sums of univariate functions). The survey frames this as a good match for the "localized, nonlinear saliency" nature of small infrared blobs.
- *U-shaped architectures (U-Net and descendants)*: Encoder compresses the image and extracts semantics; decoder upsamples back to full resolution; skip connections ferry fine spatial detail from encoder to decoder so segmentation doesn't lose localization. Problem: a small target's spatial signal is exactly what gets lost during the encoder's downsampling. Fix: "dense nested" variants (UNet++, DNA-Net) that connect many encoder/decoder depths to each other, so features at the right scale for a given target size survive somewhere in the network.
- *Loss functions*: Standard segmentation losses (MSE, BCE, Dice, Soft-IoU) treat every pixel equally. Since a target may be only 1 pixel critical (the centroid) out of a million background pixels, uniform weighting under-penalizes errors on the few pixels that matter. The survey lists dozens of reweighted/task-specific losses (focal loss variants, Wasserstein losses, boundary-aware losses) designed to emphasize target pixels or specific failure modes (centroid accuracy, boundary fidelity).
- *Learning-paradigm shortcuts*: Full pixel-level annotation of tiny targets is expensive and error-prone, so "point supervision" lets annotators mark just a centroid; the network is trained to progressively grow a pseudo pixel-mask around that point over training (e.g., LESPS: exploit the observation that CNNs' predictions naturally expand from a point toward the true mask shape during training, then use that expanding prediction as a proxy label). Weak supervision and source-free domain adaptation attack the related problems of partial labels and the model needing to work on new sensors/scenes without access to the original training data.
- *SAM adaptation*: SAM (Segment Anything Model) is a general-purpose foundation segmentation model (image encoder + prompt encoder + mask decoder) trained on natural images. Applied to infrared, it underperforms because infrared textures/statistics (noise, low contrast, foreground/background imbalance) differ sharply from SAM's training distribution; fixes involve fine-tuning with hand-crafted infrared-specific priors or injecting scene-level language priors.
- *Graph/Hypergraph Neural Networks*: Instead of treating the image as a regular pixel grid, model it as a graph of nodes (e.g., superpixels or regions) with edges encoding relationships; GNNs propagate information along these edges (spectral methods use graph-frequency theory, spatial methods aggregate local graph neighborhoods). Hypergraphs generalize edges to connect more than two nodes at once, useful for capturing higher-order relationships (e.g., a target's relation to several background regions at once).

## Key insights
- **Genuinely useful contribution**: this is a broad, up-to-date taxonomy/reference mapping of the IRSTD deep-learning literature (architectures × mechanisms × learning paradigms × evaluation), explicitly organized to let a newcomer or practitioner navigate hundreds of recent papers (tables 1–3 catalog ~dozens of named methods by category). That organizational/survey value is the paper's actual contribution.
- **No new method or experiment**: the paper does not propose a novel model or run new benchmarks itself — every performance claim ("outperforms SOTA," "AUC of 0.9977," etc.) is a reported claim from the cited original papers, not independently verified by this survey's authors.
- **Recurring cross-cutting theme worth noting**: nearly every architecture family (CNN, Transformer, Mamba, SAM) is shown to have a fundamental mismatch with the tiny/high-frequency/low-contrast nature of infrared targets (downsampling destroys signal; attention low-pass-filters it away; domain gap hurts foundation models) — the survey is useful precisely because it names these failure modes explicitly rather than just listing methods.
- **Claimed future directions** (per abstract) include promising directions for research, but the provided text (truncated) does not reach that concluding section, so the specifics of these "perspectives" are not available from the given excerpt.
