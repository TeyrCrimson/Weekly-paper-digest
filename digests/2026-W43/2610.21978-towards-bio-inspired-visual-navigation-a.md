# Towards bio-inspired visual navigation: a neuromorphic navigation paradigm for MAVs

**Authors:** Junjie Jiang, Ankang Zhou, Zheng Fang

**arXiv:** [2610.21978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21978)

**Categories:** cs.RO

**Submitted:** 2026-10-15

**Relevance:** 9/10 — matched: Onboard object detection from a moving UAV

---

## TL;DR
This paper builds the first complete end-to-end neuromorphic pipeline for MAV (micro aerial vehicle) visual navigation: an event camera feeds a spiking neural network that estimates depth, extracts navigation features, and outputs waypoints, with the navigation policy deployed on Intel's Loihi-based Kapoho Bay neuromorphic chip. It matches ANN-based navigation performance in simulation and real-world flights while cutting power consumption to ~20% (perception) and ~12% (navigation) of the ANN equivalent. The system is validated both in six simulated environments and in physical indoor/outdoor drone flights, not just simulation.

## First-principles breakdown

**The problem.** Small drones (MAVs) have tight power and compute budgets. Today's navigation stacks use regular cameras (capture full image frames at a fixed rate) and ANNs (artificial neural networks, i.e. standard deep learning) to process them. This is "dense" (every pixel processed every frame) and "synchronous" (locked to a clock), which wastes energy on redundant information — most pixels don't change between frames. Biological systems (insect/bird vision) instead sense and compute only when something actually changes, achieving far better power efficiency. The paper's bet: literally copy that structure — event-driven sensors + spike-based computation + spike-based chips — rather than just using biologically-inspired software tricks on conventional hardware.

**Event cameras.** Instead of outputting full frames, each pixel independently fires an "event" (x, y, timestamp, polarity) only when its log-brightness changes past a threshold (Eq 1–2). This yields a sparse, asynchronous stream instead of a dense raster — far less data, microsecond timing resolution, better behavior under fast motion/lighting changes.

**Spiking neural networks (SNNs).** Instead of neurons outputting continuous activation values every cycle (as in ANNs), SNN neurons accumulate input over discrete time steps into a "membrane potential" and fire a discrete spike only when that potential crosses a threshold (the Leaky Integrate-and-Fire, or LIF, model; the paper uses two LIF variants, CUBA-LIF and TS-LIF, differing in whether synaptic current and membrane voltage decay are modeled as one state or two — Eq 3–4). Because computation only happens when spikes arrive, and spikes are binary and sparse, this maps naturally onto energy-efficient neuromorphic chips (accumulate-only operations, "ACs", instead of the multiply-accumulate, "MACs", of ANNs — MACs cost ~4.6 pJ vs ~0.9 pJ for ACs under their energy model).

**Why prior neuromorphic navigation fell short.** Earlier work used event cameras but then fed ANNs (losing the computational efficiency), or used SNNs but fed them ordinary camera frames (losing the sensing efficiency) — a sensing/compute mismatch that squanders the main benefit of going neuromorphic. Other neuromorphic systems that did combine both only tackled simple subtasks (e.g., single-axis control, optical-flow-based ego-motion), not full goal-directed navigation in cluttered environments. Limiting factors: raw event streams have no explicit geometric/structural information (they only encode brightness change, not "there's a wall at 2m"), SNNs are historically hard to train end-to-end (spikes are non-differentiable, so gradients can't flow normally), and neuromorphic chips support few neuron types/operators, capping model complexity.

**This paper's mechanism, step by step:**
1. *Perception*: Convert event streams into "event frames" (accumulate events per pixel over a window, split into positive/negative-polarity channels). Feed through a spiking U-Net (encoder-decoder with skip connections, all built from LIF neurons) to predict a depth map — this is the "spiking perceptual representation module." A depth-estimation auxiliary task is used specifically to inject the missing geometric structure into the event-derived representation (fixing the "raw events lack structure" problem) and to reduce the sim-to-real gap.
2. The depth map is passed through a spiking residual network (SEW-ResNet-based) to compress it into compact geometric features (Z).
3. *Encoding continuous values into spikes*: To let an SNN process continuous quantities (velocity, acceleration, goal direction, features) within only a handful of time steps, the paper uses "population coding" — each scalar value is represented by several neurons, each with a learnable Gaussian "receptive field" (tuned to a different part of the value's range); the value's distance from each neuron's preferred value determines its firing intensity (Eq 8-10). This is the standard neuroscience trick for representing continuous signals with discrete spiking populations, now made learnable (trainable means/widths) rather than fixed.
4. *Navigation policy*: The spiking navigation network consumes these encoded spikes (state + goal + visual features) and outputs spikes that are decoded back into continuous action values via a trainable linear "population decoding" readout over firing rates (Eq 11-12) — predicting an offset to a candidate waypoint (defined in polar coordinates relative to the drone).
5. *Converting a waypoint into a flyable trajectory, differentiably*: The predicted waypoint and current state define boundary conditions for a quintic (5th-order) polynomial trajectory. Three losses are computed on this geometric trajectory: smoothness (penalizes jerky polynomial curvature), safety/collision (penalizes trajectory points close to obstacles, via a distance-based penalty function), and goal (penalizes distance from the final target). Crucially, because the trajectory is parameterized in closed form, these losses are differentiable with respect to the network's output, so gradients flow backward through the trajectory formulation into the SNN — this is "guidance learning," borrowed from prior non-spiking planners (iPlanner, YOPO) and adapted here.
6. *Training spikes*: Since the spike firing function (a hard threshold) has zero gradient almost everywhere, the authors use a "surrogate gradient" — a smooth function substituted for the true gradient during backpropagation only — standard practice for training SNNs end-to-end.
7. *Deployment*: Because the Loihi-based Kapoho Bay chip supports limited neuron models, the authors split the system: the perception module (depth + feature extraction) runs as a "simulated SNN" on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX (i.e., SNN computation emulated on conventional hardware), while the navigation policy module alone runs natively on the Kapoho Bay neuromorphic chip. The two communicate wirelessly via ROS between the drone's onboard computer and an external host tied to the Kapoho Bay (since Kapoho Bay isn't ARM-compatible/onboard-mountable).

## Key insights

- **Genuinely new**: the first demonstration of a *complete* event-camera → SNN → neuromorphic-chip pipeline for full goal-directed MAV navigation (not just pose estimation or single-axis control), validated with a physical flight system, not only simulation. This systems-integration claim is reasonably well supported — they show sim results, physical indoor/outdoor flights, and power measurements.
- **Learnable population coding + guidance learning combo**: adapting continuous-value population coding (with trainable receptive fields) and differentiable trajectory-based guidance losses to let an SNN learn a navigation policy end-to-end in only 4–5 time steps. This is a legitimate technical contribution — prior guidance-learning planners (iPlanner, YOPO) were ANN-based; adapting the differentiable trajectory loss to drive gradients through a spiking network via surrogate gradients is the paper's novel engineering synthesis, even if each individual piece (population coding, surrogate gradients, quintic trajectory losses) is borrowed from prior work.
- **Energy claims are the headline, and are reasonably concrete**: 19.57% of ANN power for the perception module (computed analytically from operation counts, not measured) and 12.37% for the navigation module on real Loihi hardware (measured with power meters) relative to ANN running on Jetson Orin NX. The hardware-measured number (navigation module) is stronger evidence than the analytically-estimated one (perception module).
- **Navigation performance parity with ANN baseline is claimed and supported** by matched success rate/trajectory distance/time across six simulated environments and three speeds (Table 1), though margins are small and the SNN is occasionally slightly worse (e.g., 45/48 vs 46/48 at 1.0 m/s).
- Ablation showing that **depth supervision is necessary** — without it the policy collapses into collisions/ground contact — is a useful, clearly demonstrated internal result (Fig 9).

## Caveats — stated and inferred

- **Partial onboard deployment, not fully embodied neuromorphic system** (stated): the Kapoho Bay chip isn't ARM-compatible, so the navigation module runs off-board and communicates wirelessly with the drone; the perception module is only a "simulated SNN" running on a conventional GPU-class chip (Jetson Orin NX), not actual neuromorphic hardware. The touted "end-to-end neuromorphic" framing is thus partially aspirational — only one of the two modules is physically running on true neuromorphic silicon.
- **Pose estimation still relies on conventional frame-based camera** (stated): the Intel RealSense T265 provides pose estimation, meaning the system is not actually all-event-camera; a core sensing function still depends on frame-based vision, undercutting the "fully neuromorphic sensing" narrative.
- **Real-world success rates are clearly worse than simulation** (stated): 7/10 indoor, 9/10 outdoor vs near-ceiling simulation success rates, attributed to limited event-camera field of view and noisy/lower-quality real depth ground truth (especially indoors). This is a meaningful sim-to-real gap acknowledged directly by the authors.
- **Small real-world trial count** (inferred): only 10 trials each for indoor/outdoor — not enough to produce statistically reliable success-rate estimates or rule out luck/cherry-picking of favorable runs.
- **ANN baseline comparability is unclear** (inferred): the "ANN-based counterpart" is created by swapping SNN modules for ANN modules of presumably matched architecture, but no details are given on whether the ANN baseline was itself tuned/optimized to its full potential, or whether it represents a strong, independently-established prior method (e.g., a published SOTA navigation network) versus simply an ablation-style internal baseline. This weakens the strength of the "comparable to ANN" claim as a comparison against the state of the art.
- **Energy comparison for the perception module is analytical, not measured** (inferred): the 19.57% figure comes from counting MAC/AC operations and applying a fixed per-operation energy cost under a 45nm CMOS assumption (a common but idealized methodology) — not an actual hardware power measurement, unlike the navigation module's Kapoho Bay numbers. This is a weaker form of evidence than it may first appear, since real hardware overheads (memory access, control logic, I/O) aren't captured.
- **No comparison against other published neuromorphic navigation baselines** (inferred): the paper only compares its SNN to its own ANN ablation, not to any other neuromorphic system from the literature (e.g., refs 41-44), even though it claims to surpass them in task complexity — that claim is qualitative/narrative, not quantitatively benchmarked.
- **Code/data not yet released** (stated): "currently being curated for public release," meaning independent verification isn't currently possible.
- **Limited environment diversity** (inferred): simulation tests use six forest-like environments generated from one simulator (Flightmare) with a fixed obstacle style (tree-trunk-like obstacles with bark textures replicated in real-world tests); generalization to structurally different environments (indoor corridors, dynamic obstacles, varied lighting beyond what's tested) is not addressed in the paper.
- **Slower-speed degradation explained but not fixed** (stated): success rate drops at low speeds due to sparse event generation from distant objects causing unreliable depth estimation — a structural limitation of event-camera-based depth sensing at low relative motion, not just a training artifact.
- **Future-work items admitted as current gaps** (stated): full onboard neuromorphic deployment and event-based (rather than frame-based) visual odometry are explicitly listed as unresolved, meaning the "complete neuromorphic navigation loop" is not yet achieved even by the authors' own account.
