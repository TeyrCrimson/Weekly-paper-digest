# Weekly summary

## The week in one paragraph
Eight digests this week split into two clusters: four papers probing hidden biases and fixes in monocular/relative depth estimation (focal-length blindness, scale-structure entanglement, unsafe overconfidence, capacity-training mismatch in small models), and four on niche UAV/drone perception tasks (PV-panel segmentation, cross-modal tracking, generative-model segmentation, wildfire detection) that each ship a new dataset, benchmark, or adaptation trick rather than a fundamentally new architecture. A clear methodological thread ties several papers together: self-supervised, label-free test-time or staged adaptation (EM-ViT's virtual stereo, TSAT's token-shift equivariance, Signal Scaffolding's phased distillation) is the field's go-to answer to domain shift this week. The standout is the digital-zoom paper, which turns a known ambiguity into a free, exact diagnostic anyone can run on a released model — most other depth papers here are better read as case studies in how deep that ambiguity's consequences run (robot collisions, Mars rover terrain, tiny edge models). Evidence quality varies sharply: the safety-navigation and PV-dataset papers have solid, well-controlled comparisons, while several others (wildfire, TSAT, DroneSegEdit, ReForgeDepth) lean on single-dataset, single-lab, or simulation-only evaluation that the digests themselves flag as caveats. Nothing this week claims or demonstrates a general-purpose fix — every contribution is narrow, tested in its own sandbox, and explicit (to its authors' credit) about where it hasn't been shown to generalize.

## Themes
- **Monocular depth's hidden biases, and narrow fixes for them**: *Zoom In, Scale Out*, *Adapting Monocular Depth Estimation... (EM-ViT)*, *From Depth to Safe Depth*, and the untitled lightweight-depth paper (ReForgeDepth/Signal Scaffolding) collectively establish that "metric" or "relative" depth outputs carry systematic, explainable biases (focal-length shrinkage, scale/structure entanglement, near-obstacle overestimation, small-model capacity mismatch) that can be diagnosed and partially corrected with targeted mechanisms — but none claims a general solution, and each fix is validated on a narrow slice (one backbone, simulation, or one dataset family).
- **Self-supervised / test-time adaptation as the default answer to domain shift**: EM-ViT (LiDAR+ego-motion self-supervision for metric scale), TSAT (token-shift equivariance + temporal backward prediction for UAV tracker modality switches), and the Signal Scaffolding paper (staged self-distillation for small depth nets) all adapt models online or in stages using signals derived from the model's own structure rather than new labels. This establishes the technique is viable across very different tasks, but each instance depends on task-specific geometric or architectural assumptions and is tested on only 1-2 extra datasets/architectures beyond its primary benchmark.
- **UAV/drone perception still runs on bespoke, narrow data**: *PVIS-10K*, *DroneSegEdit*, and the wildfire flame-detection paper each build or adapt a model for one specific drone-imagery task and show general-purpose alternatives (zero-shot SAM 3, generic non-local attention) lag well behind task-specific ones. Collectively they establish that current foundation models are not yet drop-in solutions for small-object, oblique-angle, or motion-pattern UAV tasks — but all three evaluations are confined to a single country/platform/fire site, so the "foundation models underperform" conclusion is suggestive rather than settled.

## Read these
- **Zoom In, Scale Out** — the only paper offering a free, exact, label-free diagnostic (digital zoom) that anyone can run on any released depth model, with a principled explanation (shrinkage estimation) rather than just an empirical pattern.
- **From Depth to Safe Depth** — the cleanest causal result of the week: a matched-coverage control proves *where* caution is spent (not how much) drives a 40-point navigation success swing, backed by a statistically significant ablation — directly actionable for anyone feeding monocular depth into a planner.
- **Adapting Monocular Depth Estimation (EM-ViT)** — broadest real-world evaluation among the depth papers (garage, farm, KITTI, and Mars rover Navcam on embedded hardware), with a genuinely distinct mechanism (virtual-stereo scale/structure disentanglement) rather than another fine-tuning recipe.

## Skip unless
- **A 10K drone image dataset (PVIS-10K)** — relevant only if you need component-level PV-panel instance segmentation data or are benchmarking instance-vs-coverage metrics; otherwise just a (well-built) dataset release.
- **DroneSegEdit** — matters if you're specifically interested in repurposing generative image editors for referring segmentation on small, repeated UAV targets; aggregate gains outside its specialty (compact classes) are mixed.
- **Test-Time Selective Adaptation (TSAT)** — relevant if you work on cross-modal (visible/thermal) UAV tracking specifically on or near the CM-UOT benchmark; generalization beyond the authors' own tracker/dataset is thin.
- **Obscured Wildfire Flame Detection** — worth reading only if you're working on IR-to-RGB distillation for fire detection; it's validated on one curated subset of one fire in one forest, with ground truth itself coming from a hand-tuned heuristic pipeline.
- **The untitled lightweight depth paper (ReForgeDepth/Signal Scaffolding)** — useful if you're optimizing sub-5M-parameter depth models for edge devices; gains are real but numerically modest, validated only on driving-scene datasets, and partly leverage an unpublished same-lab teacher model.
