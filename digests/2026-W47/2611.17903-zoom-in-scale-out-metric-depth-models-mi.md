# Zoom In, Scale Out: Metric Depth Models Mistake Digital Zoom for Moving Closer

**Authors:** Rabeya Noor, Khandoker Nosiba Arifin

**arXiv:** [2611.17903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17903)

**Categories:** cs.CV

**Submitted:** 2026-11-13

**Relevance:** 10/10 — matched: Metric and temporally-consistent monocular depth

---

## TL;DR
Monocular "metric depth" models secretly rely on a camera's focal length to convert pixel size into metres, and this paper shows that a trivial, label-free test — digitally zooming a photo — exposes how badly most models estimate that focal length. Pixel-only models (e.g., Depth Anything V2) treat a zoomed photo as if the camera moved closer (depth shrinks almost exactly like 1/zoom), while camera-predicting models (Depth Pro, MoGe-2, UniDepthV2) are better but still systematically under-grow their focal-length estimate under zoom. The authors give a geometric explanation (the "zoom–squash ambiguity"), a training-free fix (FocalUnshrink) that nearly cures Depth Pro, and a label-free fine-tuning recipe that further reduces the error, at a measured accuracy cost.

## First-principles breakdown

**The core problem.** To say "that object is 5 metres away" from a single 2D photo, a model implicitly needs to know the camera's focal length — the same object subtends more pixels through a telephoto lens than a wide lens at the same distance. Some models are *told* the focal length (camera intrinsics), some *predict* it from the image, and some ignore it and just guess metres straight from pixel patterns (e.g., "cars are usually ~4.5m long, this one spans X pixels, so it must be Y metres away"). Benchmarks can't distinguish these strategies because test sets use only a handful of fixed cameras — a model that memorizes "KITTI's camera" can score well without ever reasoning about focal length at all.

**The test: digital zoom.** Cropping an image and resizing the crop back to full resolution is mathematically identical to having used a camera with a longer focal length, shooting from the exact same spot (Eq. 1 derives the exact new focal length and principal point). Critically: nothing in the 3D scene moved. So every surviving pixel's true distance is unchanged, and the "correct" camera's focal length should scale up exactly by the zoom factor `s`. This gives a ground-truth-free check: feed a model the zoomed image and see if its depth output stays the same and its predicted focal length scales by `s`. No labels, no new data, exact geometry.

**The two numbers.** They summarize a model's response to zoom with two power-law exponents fit in log-log space:
- α: how much predicted depth shrinks, as `s^-α`. Ideal α=0 (depth unchanged). α=1 would mean "model thinks it walked exactly proportionally closer."
- β: how much predicted focal length grows, as `s^β`. Ideal β=1 (focal grows exactly with zoom).

**Why size-based reasoning fails — the "zoom–squash ambiguity."** The paper's key geometric insight: a zoomed photo of a scene is pixel-for-pixel *identical* to an unzoomed photo of that same scene physically squashed (compressed) along the depth axis by factor `s`. Lateral/width cues look the same either way — only depth extent and angles differ. So any model that infers distance mainly from "how big does this familiar object look" cannot distinguish "camera zoomed in" from "scene got squashed closer," and it will land on the line α+β=1 (preserving apparent lateral size, but getting both depth and 3D shape wrong). This is shown to be where Depth Pro sits per-image.

**Why focal-length predictors systematically undershoot (β<1).** The authors model a focal estimator trained by regression as a Bayesian shrinkage estimator: if training cameras have log-focal distributed as N(μ,τ²) and the image gives noisy evidence of the true focal, the squared-error-optimal prediction is a weighted average pulling toward the training distribution's mean μ. The weight ρ=τ²/(τ²+σ²) is exactly the measured β — "how reliable is the apparent-size cue." Since no cue is perfectly reliable, trained estimators always play it safe and under-respond to real focal changes, especially large zooms (confirmed empirically: β degrades from ~0.83 to ~0.65 for Depth Pro moving from small to large zooms).

**Locating the fault: give the model the true camera.** To test whether the depth network or the focal-estimation head is the culprit, the authors literally substitute the ground-truth (zoomed) focal length into Depth Pro and DA3-Nested's pipelines (both compute metric depth as focal × a camera-free internal output, so this substitution is an exact rescaling). Result: α drops to ~0, proving the depth network itself is fine — all the error lives in the focal-length head. For MoGe-2 and UniDepthV2, which take the camera as an architectural input, re-running with true camera also improves accuracy, not just equivariance.

**FocalUnshrink — a training-free fix.** Since the bias is a known shrinkage-toward-the-mean effect, it can be algebraically inverted: measure β via self-zoom (no labels), measure μ as the mean unzoomed log-focal on the target domain's images, then correct via `log_f_corrected = μ + (log f_predicted − μ)/β`. This undoes the "playing it safe" behavior. For Depth Pro, this takes α from 0.31 to 0.04 at negligible cost on normal (unzoomed) images — though it amplifies noise more for models with less reliable focal cues (low β), per a measured accuracy/equivariance tradeoff.

**Label-free fine-tuning (EFT+B).** Beyond the training-free fix, they fine-tune Depth Anything V2 using a self-consistency loss: a frozen "teacher" copy of the model gives a target depth map, and the "student" is trained so its prediction on a randomly zoomed crop matches the teacher's prediction on the original (resampled onto the crop grid), plus an anchor term keeping the student's unzoomed output close to the teacher's. Naively this works but the model discovers a shortcut: zoomed images are blurrier (lower effective resolution after upsampling), so the model learns "blurry = zoomed" rather than real geometric reasoning — revealed by the "blur control" (down-then-upsample with no actual field-of-view change) causing large depth shifts post-finetuning. Adding a third loss term that forces blur-only inputs to still predict the *original* unzoomed depth removes this shortcut, pushing α down to 0.05–0.07 on training domains, generalizing partially (worse) to unseen datasets, with a measured hit to normal-image accuracy (AbsRel).

## Key insights

- **Genuinely new**: Reframing the classical focal-depth ambiguity as an *exact, testable* geometric transform (digital zoom) applicable to any released model, with no labels needed — this is a practical audit tool, not just a theoretical observation.
- **Genuinely new**: The α+β=1 line as a diagnostic signature of apparent-size-based reasoning, and the derivation connecting β directly to "how reliable the camera cue is" via a shrinkage-estimator argument — this gives a principled explanation (not just an empirical pattern) for *why* focal heads under-scale.
- **Genuinely new**: The "give the model the true camera" intervention cleanly isolates fault to the focal-head vs. the depth-network for architectures that factor depth = focal × camera-free-output — a clever, nearly free way to localize the error source.
- **Useful, practical**: FocalUnshrink is label-free, training-free, and makes Depth Pro pass the test almost for free — a deployable fix, not just a diagnosis.
- **Claimed vs. evidenced**: The claim that "bigger/newer models don't fix this" is evidenced only on a modest sample (up to 151 shared images for the largest models), and the result is directional (size helps a little, camera-awareness matters much more) rather than a definitive scaling law.
- **Evidence supports but is narrower than it might seem**: the label-free α score is explicitly shown to predict *degradation under zoom*, not general accuracy (correlation 0.67 with added error vs. -0.09 with baseline accuracy) — the authors are careful to state this distinction themselves, which is good practice worth flagging as an evidence-scope caveat rather than a flaw.

## Caveats — stated and inferred

- **(stated)** Real optical zoom validation uses only 50 scenes from a single lens (SR-RAW dataset) — a small, narrow confirmation of the digital-zoom findings.
- **(stated)** When the camera is not given to the model, a crop also removes surrounding context (not just causing magnification), so α=0 is described by the authors as "a goal, not a promise" — the test measures deviation and direction, not a clean ground truth.
- **(stated)** The "give model the true camera" experiment is only exact (an algebraic rescaling) for Depth Pro and DA3-Nested; for MoGe-2 and UniDepthV2 it requires a full model rerun and is a less clean intervention.
- **(stated)** EFT+B (the fine-tuned fix) measurably lowers accuracy (AbsRel) on normal, unzoomed photos and on unseen datasets/domains — the authors report this cost rather than hide it.
- **(stated)** FocalUnshrink is explicitly called "a first-order correction": it assumes a Gaussian-shrinkage structure, needs unlabeled target-domain images to calibrate its scale, and needs a reasonably reliable focal cue (low-β models get worse, not better, from the correction due to noise amplification).
- **(stated)** EFT-F (focal-head-only tuning for Depth Pro) does not transfer to datasets it wasn't trained on — α actually increases on unseen DIODE data.
- **(inferred)** All evaluation is on standard academic depth benchmarks (KITTI, NYUv2, DIODE) — real-world deployment conditions (compression artifacts, varied sensors, non-pinhole lenses, video) are only partially covered via supplementary controls (JPEG, bilinear, blur), so generalization to arbitrary in-the-wild photos is not fully established.
- **(inferred)** Sample sizes for the newest/largest models (DA3-Nested, VGGT, UniK3D) are small (as few as 50-78-151 shared images), which is a relatively weak basis for claims about how "bigger and newer models" behave — results here should be read as suggestive rather than conclusive.
- **(inferred)** The fine-tuning experiments use only one small backbone (Depth Anything V2 Small, 24.8M params) and train on limited data (a few thousand images, one GPU) — whether the "blurry means zoomed" shortcut and its fix generalize to larger architectures or different training regimes is not addressed in the paper.
- **(inferred)** The paper's own authorship note states LLMs (Claude and ChatGPT) wrote and ran experiment code, analyzed results, and drafted the text under "limited step-by-step direction" from the human authors — this doesn't invalidate the findings, but it is a notable methodological detail about how much of the empirical pipeline was AI-generated versus human-verified, left for the reader to weigh.
