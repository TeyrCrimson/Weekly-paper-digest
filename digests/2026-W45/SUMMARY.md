# Weekly summary

## The week in one paragraph
Five papers this week split into two clusters: LLM-agent security (indirect prompt injection, attack and defense) and perception models being pushed into domains far from their training distribution (lunar terrain, aerial drone footage, synthetic-to-real depth). The security pair is the most self-contained story of the week — one paper shows *where* an injected instruction sits matters as much as what it says, the other builds a structural defense that nearly eliminates that exact attack class, though both explicitly leave direct and multi-turn injection largely unsolved. The perception papers share a weaker, more exploratory character: each asks whether a model trained on one domain (terrestrial photos, synthetic indoor scenes) transfers to another (the Moon, drone footage, robot workbenches), with promising numbers but thin or qualitative-only evidence for the headline claims. Nothing this week is a finished, deployment-ready result — the strongest claims (LLMON's ASR drop, the trust region effect) are well-controlled, but every paper's edge cases (rare classes, direct attacks, scale recovery, robotic-scene generalization) are acknowledged as open or untested. Read-worthiness this week favors the security cluster over the perception cluster.

## Themes

- **Prompt injection is about geography as much as wording** — *Where You Inject Matters* and *Post-Training Large Language Models with LLMON* are two sides of the same coin: the first shows an identical malicious payload succeeds up to 2.6× more often depending on which file/surface and position it's placed in, and builds a placement-aware RL attacker to exploit this; the second shows a structural, reserved-token "execution binding" scheme can drive indirect-injection success down to near zero while making the same move legible from a defender's side (per-layer attention metrics). Together they establish that trust in agent contexts is a positional/structural phenomenon exploitable by attackers and partially fixable by defenders — but both papers agree direct and multi-turn injection remain only modestly addressed (robustness capped around 28–37% in LLMON's own numbers).
- **Generative/world models as a cheap substitute for real training data** — *Dreams Are Cheaper Than Flights* and *FlowMoDE* both lean on generative models (a video world model; a flow-matching depth decoder) to stand in for expensive real-world capture or costly diffusion inference. Both report real gains (4–33% mAP lift from synthetic aerial frames; state-of-the-art depth accuracy at a fraction of generative-baseline compute) but both also flag that their best-sounding claims — "20x cheaper," "generalizes to robotic scenes" — rest on single-dataset, qualitative, or assumption-heavy evidence (per-class collapse on rare classes; robotic-scene results shown only in figures, no metrics).
- **Terrestrial-trained vision backbones generalize further than expected, but scale/fidelity remain open** — the lunar depth-estimation paper and FlowMoDE both find that ViT/DINOv2-based models trained with no exposure to the target domain (lunar regolith; the specific real test scenes) still produce structurally coherent depth, outperforming domain-specific baselines (lunar-tuned stereogrammetry; prior generative depth models). Neither establishes metric/absolute accuracy or end-task validation — the lunar paper explicitly leaves scale recovery and operator-task benefit untested, and FlowMoDE's analogous robotic-generalization claim is qualitative only.

## Read these
- **Post-Training Large Language Models with LLMON** — the week's most rigorously evidenced result (third-party-benchmark ASR drop from 53–100% to 0.02–5.6%, backed by mechanistic attention analysis), and it directly completes the story the injection-attack paper starts.
- **Where You Inject Matters: The Trust Region Effect in LLM Agents** — a cleanly controlled (content-held-fixed) causal finding about agent security that reframes how injection attacks and defenses should be designed; read alongside LLMON for the full attack/defense picture.
- **Efficacy of Monocular Depth Estimation in Lunar Environments** — the most surprising generalization result of the week (terrestrial ViT depth models beating a lunar-tuned stereo baseline), with real LiDAR ground truth behind the headline numbers rather than purely qualitative checks.

## Skip unless
- **Dreams Are Cheaper Than Flights** — relevant only if you need a cost/accuracy case study for using world-model-generated synthetic data in aerial detection; the cost-savings and "synthetic beats real" claims rest on one dataset family, a single human curator, and assumption-heavy pricing, with a demonstrated per-class collapse (bus AP 0.211→0.010) that aggregate metrics hide.
- **FlowMoDE: Coarse-to-fine Flow Matching for Detail-aware Sim-to-Real Monocular Depth Estimation** — worth a look only if you specifically need an efficient generative depth estimator; the core contribution (coarse-to-fine + semantic conditioning on flow matching) is incremental over existing flow-matching depth work, and its most eye-catching claim (robotic-scene generalization) has no quantitative backing.
