# FlowMoDE: Coarse-to-fine Flow Matching for Detail-aware Sim-to-Real Monocular Depth Estimation

**Authors:** Liangjing Shao, Wanhao Liu, Jinsong Lin, Zhiwei Fang, Hongliang Ren

**arXiv:** [2610.41190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41190)

**Categories:** cs.CV

**Submitted:** 2026-10-30

**Relevance:** 6/10 — matched: Metric and temporally-consistent monocular depth

---

## TL;DR
FlowMoDE is a flow-matching generative model for monocular depth estimation that replaces costly multi-step diffusion with a 2-step coarse-to-fine decoding scheme guided by frozen semantic features (DINOv2). Trained only on one synthetic indoor dataset (Hypersim), it beats prior discriminative and generative depth models on five real-world benchmarks (indoor+outdoor) while using far less compute than other generative baselines. It also shows qualitatively better depth/point-cloud reconstruction on robotic scenes with cables and thin structures.

## First-principles breakdown

**The problem.** Monocular depth estimation (MDE) predicts a per-pixel depth map from a single RGB image — a core building block for robot navigation, manipulation, and 3D reconstruction. Two families of methods exist:

- **Discriminative models** (e.g., DPT, Depth Anything, MoGe, UniDepth): a Vision Transformer encoder compresses the image into a coarse grid of tokens, and a convolutional decoder upsamples that into a depth map. Because the encoder throws away fine pixel-level detail during tokenization (to get global semantic context cheaply), the decoder can never fully recover sharp edges, wires, or distant small objects — the information is simply gone. These models also need huge labeled datasets to generalize.
- **Generative models** (e.g., Marigold, PPD, DepthMaster): repurpose image diffusion models (like Stable Diffusion) to "denoise" a depth map conditioned on the image. Diffusion models iteratively refine a noisy sample over many steps, which lets them reconstruct sharper structural detail, but at a steep computational cost (many forward passes). Many of these also use a VAE to compress depth into a latent space before diffusing, which — like the ViT tokenization problem above — loses fine edge detail. Attempts to speed this up with single-step diffusion sacrifice depth-map fidelity.

**Flow matching basics.** Flow matching is an alternative to standard denoising diffusion. Instead of learning to reverse a stochastic noising process over hundreds of steps, you learn a "velocity field" v_θ(x_t, t) that describes a smooth ODE path transporting a sample from noise (p0) to the real data distribution (p1). The straight-line interpolation is x_t = (1-t)x0 + t·x1, and training just regresses the predicted velocity against the fixed target velocity (x1 - x0) via MSE. Because the path is simple (linear), you can often integrate it in very few steps (even 1-2) at inference, unlike classic diffusion's hundreds of steps.

**FlowMoDE's mechanism, step by step:**
1. **Semantic conditioning**: A frozen pretrained semantic encoder (DINOv2-B, borrowed from MoGe) extracts global image features. A lightweight "Semantic Decoder" (4 transformer blocks) turns these into a compact condition vector — giving the model global scene context without re-learning a huge encoder from scratch.
2. **Noise/time setup**: The ground-truth depth map is normalized and mixed with Gaussian noise at a sampled timestep t (Eq. 4-5), concatenated with the input image.
3. **Coarse Decoder**: Takes the noisy depth+image patches (16×16 patch size) and the semantic+time condition (injected via "modulation" — scale/shift/gate factors applied to attention and MLP layers, similar to AdaLN in diffusion transformers) and produces a coarse feature map.
4. **Fine-grained Decoder**: The coarse output is upsampled (to 8×8 patches, i.e., finer resolution) and passed through another conditioned transformer stack to recover high-frequency structural details (edges, thin objects) that the coarse stage couldn't represent.
5. **Velocity output**: A final modulated linear layer unpatchifies the fine feature map into the predicted velocity field, which is the thing the model is trained to match to the true target velocity (ϵ - depth).
6. **Two-step inference**: Despite being trained with a single-step velocity-matching objective, at inference the same shared network is applied twice: first to go from pure noise to an intermediate halfway point x_0.5, then again from x_0.5 to the final clean depth estimate. This "coarse-to-fine, few-step" trick is the core efficiency lever — ablations (Table V) show 2 steps is actually the sweet spot, better than 1, 4, 6, or 8 steps.

The net effect: semantic features give global scene understanding (fixing the generative models' weak global context problem), while operating directly in pixel/patch space with explicit fine decoding layers (rather than a lossy VAE latent) preserves structural detail (fixing the discriminative models' edge-detail problem) — and doing this in 2 steps instead of dozens keeps compute low.

## Key insights

- **Claimed and supported**: Combining semantic priors with a coarse-to-fine, few-step flow-matching decoder lets a generative depth model match or beat state-of-the-art discriminative models on accuracy while needing far fewer inference steps/FLOPs than prior generative models (Table I, VI: ~3140 GFLOPs and 53.4ms vs PPD's 18159 GFLOPs/193.7ms). This is backed by direct quantitative comparison across five datasets and a computational-cost table.
- **Claimed and supported**: Avoiding a VAE and working in pixel/patch space with dedicated fine-grained decoding layers improves structural/point-cloud fidelity — supported by the chamfer-distance/accuracy results (Table II) and qualitative figures showing fewer "flying points" around thin structures (cables, faucets).
- **Claimed, partially supported**: Strong sim-to-real and cross-scene generalization from training on just one synthetic indoor dataset (Hypersim, 54K frames) — supported quantitatively on 5 standard benchmarks, but the robotic-scene generalization claim (Baxter/Franka/Kuka/Da Vinci) is only shown qualitatively (Figs. 5-6), not quantitatively.
- **Useful finding from ablations**: 2-step inference outperforms both 1-step and more-step (4/6/8) variants — a non-obvious, non-monotonic result suggesting there's a real tradeoff rather than "more steps always better." Also, their specific modulation-based semantic injection beats cross-attention, concatenation, and AdaNorm alternatives (Table III), and removing semantic conditioning entirely causes huge error increases (ablation XI) — strong evidence the semantic prior is the key ingredient, not just the flow-matching mechanism itself.
- **Novelty is incremental**: flow matching for depth estimation already exists (DepthFM, cited); this paper's actual contribution is the coarse-to-fine architecture plus semantic-feature injection on top of flow matching, not flow matching itself.

## Caveats — stated and inferred

- **stated**: "patch embedding in our flow-matching pipeline may produce occasional flying pixels." (authors' own conclusion)
- **stated**: "training only on near-field indoor synthetic data combined with patch-wise denoising can cause blurred edges for distant regions" — i.e., acknowledged weakness on far-field/distant scene detail despite otherwise being the headline strength (structural detail).
- **stated**: Model has higher latency and parameter count than UniDepth v2 (a discriminative baseline) — the efficiency claim is only relative to other generative models, not universally best.
- **inferred**: Robotic-scene generalization (the paper's third claimed contribution) is evaluated only qualitatively via figures (Figs. 5-6), with no quantitative metrics, ground truth, or error bars — this is the weakest-evidenced claim in the paper despite being prominently advertised.
- **inferred**: All quantitative evaluation is on standard academic benchmarks (NYUv2, KITTI, ETH3D, ScanNet, DIODE) resized to a fixed long-edge resolution following Marigold's protocol; no evaluation on dynamic scenes, video consistency, or failure-case analysis (e.g., reflective/transparent surfaces) is presented.
- **inferred**: DIODE evaluation note states some competing works (e.g., PPD) use Sobel+RANSAC filtering/alignment while this paper uses official masks directly — a protocol difference across methods that could bias cross-method comparability on that dataset, though the paper is transparent about the discrepancy.
- **inferred**: The semantic encoder (DINOv2-B) is frozen and reused/borrowed from a prior work (MoGe), meaning the "trained exclusively on a single synthetic dataset" claim applies only to the decoder/flow-matching components, not the full pipeline — the semantic backbone itself was pretrained on much larger, likely real-world data elsewhere.
- **inferred**: No comparison of training compute/data cost is given beyond "54K" frames count in Table I; no ablation isolates how much of the performance gain comes from reusing MoGe's pretrained DINOv2 encoder versus the proposed architecture itself.
- **inferred**: All experiments run on a single NVIDIA H100; no discussion of robustness across hardware, batch sizes, or statistical variance (single run per setting, no error bars/seeds reported).
