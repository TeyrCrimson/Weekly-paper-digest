# Which Depth Estimator for Jetson AGX Orin? A 2026 Benchmark of Monocular and Stereo Methods

**Authors:** Leaf Jiang

**arXiv:** [2610.27199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27199)

**Categories:** cs.RO, cs.CV

**Submitted:** 2026-10-19

**Relevance:** 7/10 — matched: Metric and temporally-consistent monocular depth

---

## TL;DR
The author benchmarks nine depth-estimation model families (6 monocular foundation models, 3 stereo methods) on a Jetson AGX Orin, measuring accuracy against Middlebury 2014 ground truth, GPU latency, energy, and licensing across multiple input resolutions. Stereo methods (ESS, libSGM, Fast-FoundationStereo) are dramatically more accurate than every monocular model (0.33–1.18% AbsRel vs. the monocular best of 3.8–12%), and crucially, stereo error shrinks with resolution while monocular error does not (or worsens) because monocular metric scale drifts outside training resolution. The paper also shows monocular models smear 17–46% of edge pixels between foreground/background ("flying points") that the standard AbsRel metric barely detects, and gives per-frame-rate-budget deployment recommendations.

## First-principles breakdown

**The problem.** Robots/vehicles need dense metric depth (actual distances in meters, not just relative ordering) from cameras mounted on an embedded GPU (Jetson AGX Orin). Two competing approaches exist: (1) monocular depth networks that infer depth from a single image using learned priors, and (2) stereo methods that triangulate depth geometrically from two calibrated cameras. Picking between them is hard because published numbers come from different GPUs, precisions, input resolutions, and often use evaluation tricks (aligning predictions to ground truth) that hide real-world error.

**Why stereo and monocular differ fundamentally.** Stereo works by matching the same point in two images taken from slightly different viewpoints (a known "baseline" distance apart). The horizontal pixel shift between the two views (disparity, d) plus the camera's focal length (f) and baseline (B) gives depth via Z = fB/(d+offset) — this is pure geometry/triangulation, an actual *measurement*. Monocular depth, by contrast, has no geometric measurement available from a single image — the network must *infer* scale from learned priors about typical object/scene sizes seen during training. This is why, as the authors show, monocular accuracy does not improve with more pixels (more resolution only gives more detail, not more scale information), while stereo keeps improving because finer pixels mean finer disparity measurement.

**How each method works, briefly:**
- *libSGM*: classical algorithm — computes a per-pixel matching cost (census transform, a local binary pattern comparison) between left/right images, aggregates this cost along four directions to encourage smoothness, then picks the best disparity per pixel with consistency checks. No learning involved.
- *ESS / Fast-FoundationStereo*: neural networks trained to predict disparity directly from stereo pairs, much more accurate than classical SGM but costlier; Fast-FoundationStereo is a distilled (compressed), faster version of a bigger "FoundationStereo" model.
- *Monocular models* (Depth Anything V2/3, Depth Pro, MoGe-2, Metric3D v2, UniDepth V2): large vision transformers (ViT, built on DINOv2 features) trained on massive image datasets to predict either relative depth, or metric depth using tricks like a predicted/known focal length, a canonical camera space, or a global per-image scale token to convert their internal depth representation into real-world meters.

**Experimental method.** Every model is converted to a TensorRT FP16 engine (or run natively) on one Jetson Orin, fed images at up to 3 resolutions (native training size, ~2x, and full 5.7MP), and scored against real structured-light ground truth from the Middlebury 2014 stereo dataset (10 scenes chosen because their disparity stays under 256px, the search range of libSGM). Metrics: AbsRel (mean relative depth error), δ<1.05 (fraction within 5% of truth), bad-t (% of pixels off by >t px), GPU time, and energy. Monocular models are generously given the *true* focal length/intrinsics and, in a separate measurement, an *oracle* per-image scale correction (an upper bound no real system could achieve) to isolate scale error from shape error.

**Key finding mechanism — resolution scaling.** As image resolution increases: stereo's disparity measurement becomes finer-grained relative to the scene, so triangulation error shrinks (not perfectly — matching noise per pixel grows somewhat, likely because the fixed matching window covers less of the scene at higher res). Monocular networks, trained at a fixed resolution (hundreds of thousands of pixels), see position embeddings "stretched" far beyond training range when fed 5.7MP images; their learned scale prior becomes unreliable and their estimated metric scale drifts (e.g., Depth Anything V2 goes from predicting scenes 1.45x too close at native resolution to 2.4x too close at full resolution).

**Depth discontinuities / "flying points."** At object edges, correct behavior is a sharp jump between foreground/background depth. Monocular networks, having learned smooth/plausible-looking scene priors, interpolate across these edges, producing spurious points floating in between (visualized in Fig. 2) — 17–46% of edge pixels for monocular models vs 1–9% for stereo methods. This error is averaged away by AbsRel because edge pixels are only ~1.7% of all scored pixels, so it's invisible in headline accuracy numbers but matters a lot for tasks like obstacle detection or grasping.

## Key insights

- **Genuinely new**: A controlled, same-hardware, same-ground-truth, same-protocol comparison of 9 model families at multiple resolutions with TensorRT-deployed latency/energy/licensing data — this kind of apples-to-apples embedded benchmark reportedly didn't exist before (claimed, plausible given related-work discussion).
- **Resolution-scaling asymmetry** (stereo improves, monocular doesn't/worsens) is presented as a finding with a proposed causal mechanism (scale prior invalid outside training resolution; shape doesn't sharpen even with oracle scaling) — well-supported by the data (Table 3, Fig. 1) within this dataset's scope.
- **AbsRel blindness to edge errors** is a useful methodological point: a dense, smooth, low-average-error monocular depth map can still massively misplace object boundaries, something a mean-error metric hides. This is evidence-supported via a separate edge-focused analysis (Table 4, Fig. 2).
- **Cost model** translating GPU-time into "fraction of an Orin" and break-even camera price (Section 7.1) is a genuinely practical contribution for engineers deciding camera/compute tradeoffs — though it rests on the author's own compute-cost assumptions (fractional sharing, fixed Jetson pricing) rather than empirical deployment data.
- **Claimed vs. supported**: The claim "stereo is better for metric depth on Orin" is well-supported for the tested scenes (indoor, close-range, 1.3–7.6m, large baseline/focal length). The stronger implicit claim that this generalizes to all robotics/driving contexts (e.g., long-range outdoor automotive) is explicitly *not* supported — the authors flag this themselves as a scope limitation.

## Caveats — stated and inferred

- **Indoor, close-range-only test scenes (1.3–7.6m)** — stated. Authors explicitly note stereo error grows quadratically with range and that a long-baseline automotive/outdoor benchmark with LiDAR ground truth is needed; results may not generalize to long-range driving scenarios.
- **Scene selection restricted to 10 of 23 Middlebury scenes** (those with disparity <256px, matching libSGM's search range) — stated, with the authors arguing this only removes a "configuration problem with a known fix" but also acknowledging it removes the closest-range scenes where stereo is most accurate, which could favor stereo in the comparison.
- **Monocular models given information unavailable in real deployment**: true focal length/intrinsics, a domain-matched indoor fine-tuned checkpoint (Depth Anything V2 Hypersim), and in some results an oracle per-image scale upper bound — stated explicitly by authors as not realistic for deployment, intentionally generous to monocular methods to make the comparison fair in the other direction.
- **libSGM's smoothness parameters (P1, P2) were tuned on the evaluation scenes themselves** — stated; the authors ran a held-out cross-validation check showing only a 0.01–0.08 percentage point AbsRel difference, arguing this doesn't change conclusions, but it's still a form of test-set tuning not applied to the learned stereo or monocular models.
- **Conflict of interest**: author is employed by a stereo-vision company (NODAR) with a financial interest in stereo winning this comparison — stated. They exclude their own product and release all code/configs for transparency, but the inherent incentive remains.
- **Possible training-data contamination** — stated as a known unknown: "We cannot rule out that Middlebury images appear in any model's training data; if they do, that would favour the networks."
- **Timing methodology slightly favors monocular networks** — stated: network timings exclude image resizing/normalization/post-processing (and for MoGe-2, the metric-scale head), while SGM timings include host-GPU image copy overhead.
- **FP16 numerical issues required workarounds for several models** (Depth Anything 3, MoGe-2, Metric3D v2 TensorRT export bugs) — stated, and the authors mitigate by reporting accuracy from FP32 and speed from FP16, but this means the "speed" and "accuracy" numbers for several monocular models are not from the exact same execution, a methodological inconsistency the authors flag themselves.
- **No evaluation of Orin's dedicated hardware stereo (VPI/OFA), the original FoundationStereo, RAFT-Stereo, or tile-based high-resolution monocular methods (PatchFusion/PatchRefiner)** — stated as explicit coverage gaps; the tile-based methods in particular might address the exact resolution-scaling weakness the paper highlights for monocular models, so their absence leaves open whether the monocular conclusion fully generalizes.
- **Single-paper, single-author, non-peer-reviewed arXiv preprint (as of Oct 2026)** — inferred; no indication of peer review, and benchmark-style papers from vendors in the space they're evaluating carry inherent risk of framing choices that favor their business interest even when methodology is transparent.
- **No ablation on alternative ground-truth sources or sensor noise models** beyond Middlebury's structured-light setup — inferred; conclusions rest entirely on one dataset family (Middlebury 2014), so cross-dataset robustness (e.g., KITTI, outdoor, dynamic scenes) is not addressed in the paper.
