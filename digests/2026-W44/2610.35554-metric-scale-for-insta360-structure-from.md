# Metric Scale for Insta360 Structure-from-Motion from the Camera's Embedded IMU

**Authors:** Davide Cuttini, Diego Di Tommaso, Alessio Antoniacomi, Luca Simonetti

**arXiv:** [2610.35554](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35554)

**Categories:** cs.CV

**Submitted:** 2026-10-26

**Relevance:** 9/10 — matched: Metric and temporally-consistent monocular depth

---

## TL;DR
The paper replaces the smartphone-based metric-scale anchor used in a commercial 360° SfM pipeline with the Insta360 camera's own embedded IMU, recovering absolute scale via linear visual-inertial alignment over the camera's offline SfM poses. The key engineering fix is to solve the scale/gravity/bias alignment per contiguous motion segment rather than jointly over a whole survey, which removes a 3.4% systematic overestimate. Against LiDAR ground truth the resulting IMU-based scale is more than twice as accurate (0.81% MAE) as the existing smartphone reference (1.94% MAE), suggesting the extra device can be dropped.

## First-principles breakdown

**The core problem: SfM has no ruler.** Structure-from-motion reconstructs a 3D scene and camera path from 2D video by triangulating matched features across frames. This recovers everything *up to scale* — the reconstruction could be a tiny diorama or an actual building, and the images look identical either way (a "similarity transform" ambiguity: rotation, translation, and an unknown scalar all fit the data equally well). To get real-world metric units (meters), you need one external measurement of true size. The existing fix: have the operator also carry a smartphone that uses ARKit/ARCore (which fuse the phone's own IMU + camera to get metric-scale poses), snap a short burst of photos, and register those metric phone-poses against the fisheye reconstruction to transfer scale. This paper's question: can the IMU *already inside the Insta360 camera* substitute for that second device?

**Why an IMU can supply scale.** An accelerometer measures physical specific force in real units (m/s²). If you take an SfM trajectory (in made-up "model units") and compute its second derivative (acceleration), that signal has some shape, but an unknown scale factor relating model-units to meters. There is exactly one scalar that makes the model-derived acceleration (after subtracting gravity) match the real accelerometer readings. Finding that scalar — jointly with gravity direction, sensor bias, and velocity at each frame — is a classic "visual-inertial initialization" problem from SLAM (Martinelli, VINS-Mono, ORB-SLAM3 lineage). Normally this is done online over a few seconds at the start of tracking; here it's done offline, in batch, over a whole multi-minute survey, with sparse camera frames (few per second) but a continuous 1kHz IMU stream.

**Step 1 — Figure out how the IMU and camera relate.** Before you can fuse camera motion with IMU readings, you need: (a) the fixed rotation between the IMU's axes and the camera's axes (R_CB), and (b) precisely how IMU timestamps map to video frame indices (an affine model t = a·i + b, since the nominal/manufacturer frame interval is not precise enough — a 0.1% timing error misaligns video and IMU samples by more than a frame over a 10-minute survey). Both are estimated *from the data itself*, with no calibration target: they compare SfM-derived inter-frame rotations against gyroscope-integrated rotations, searching over candidate timing parameters and solving the camera-IMU rotation via Kabsch alignment (a standard closed-form "best rotation between two point sets" method). A clever diagnostic: estimate the timing offset separately in the first vs. last quarter of a survey — if they disagree, the timing model is wrong, even without ground truth.

**Step 2 — Characterize the sensor.** They check noise levels (via spectral analysis), whether video and IMU timestamps are truly synced (by comparing visual-only rotation estimates to gyro-derived rotation, finding the two already align within ~2ms), accelerometer scale accuracy (correct to 0.03% against local gravity, measured on a stationary camera), and bias drift over time (small, ~0.042 m/s² drift over 10 minutes — ruled out as the cause of larger errors seen later).

**Step 3 — The linear alignment itself.** For each inter-frame interval, integrate the accelerometer twice (once for velocity change, twice for position change) using a technique called "preintegration" (Lupton & Sukkarieh; Forster et al.) — essentially precomputing how much the accelerometer readings would move you, expressed on the model's orientation frame, independent of the as-yet-unknown scale. This produces two equations per frame-interval linear in the unknowns (scale s, gravity g, accelerometer bias b_a, and per-frame velocities). Stack these across all intervals and solve as one large sparse linear system — a standard, well-understood SLAM-initialization technique, just applied offline/in-batch rather than online.

**Step 4 — The paper's actual fix: don't share nuisance parameters across the whole survey.** The "obvious" approach solves one system for the entire survey, treating gravity and accelerometer bias as constant throughout (physically reasonable assumptions). But empirically this *overestimates* scale by 3.4% on average, consistently. The reasoning: the gravity term enters the position equation at the same magnitude as the displacement itself, so if the "constant gravity direction in model frame" assumption is even slightly violated (e.g. due to subtle orientation drift accumulated in the SfM reconstruction over minutes), that modeling error gets silently absorbed into the scale estimate instead of being visible as a residual. The fix: split the survey into independent "contiguous runs" (continuous sequences of frames with no big gaps), solve the linear system separately per run (own scale, gravity, bias, velocities), and average the per-run scales. This empirically removes the bias (MAE drops from 3.43% to 0.85%). There's a bias-variance tradeoff: runs too short are noise-dominated and bias scale low; runs too long re-inherit the joint-system's high bias. Empirically-observed contiguous runs (15-60 frame-intervals) sit near the sweet spot, confirmed by an ablation sweeping window length/overlap/stride explicitly.

**Step 5 — Quality/failure detection without ground truth.** Since there's no ground truth at deployment, they build internal self-checks: recovered gravity magnitude should be ~9.81 m/s² (if not, something's broken); scale from first-half vs second-half of the trajectory should roughly agree (large disagreement = scale drift); dispersion across per-run estimates gives a built-in confidence interval; outlier intervals are iteratively removed and re-solved.

## Key insights

- **Genuinely new and well-supported**: The core empirical finding — that naively sharing gravity/bias across a long batch SfM+IMU alignment introduces a systematic, one-sided scale bias (3.4%), and that simply not sharing those parameters (segment-wise + average) removes it — is well-demonstrated across multiple independent datasets (LiDAR, fiducial markers, public benchmark with synthetic scale injection) and backed by a solid ablation isolating the effect to "not sharing parameters" rather than any specific windowing scheme.
- **Supported by strong, varied evidence**: the IMU-derived scale is more accurate than the existing smartphone-based reference against an independent LiDAR ground truth (0.81% vs 1.94% MAE), and is shown to be unbiased on a public benchmark with trajectories artificially rescaled (mean error −0.07%), which is a nice sanity check that the method isn't just curve-fit to its own data regime.
- **Useful negative result**: monocular metric-depth networks (MoGe-2/3) are evaluated as an alternative, cheaper scale source and shown to have scene-dependent biases up to ~20%, with the two network variants disagreeing by up to 34% on the same scene — a clear "this doesn't work at the needed precision" finding, which is a legitimate contribution (ruling out an alternative) rather than a core positive claim.
- **Overclaim risk / weaker-supported claim**: the conclusion that the smartphone capture step "can therefore be removed" is a practical recommendation, but the paper itself admits (Section 6.2) that on 50 production surveys the IMU and smartphone scales disagree by up to several % with the discrepancy's source "unattributed" — i.e., in the regime without a third ground-truth reference, they cannot actually prove the IMU is better, only that it's plausibly comparable or somewhat better.
- **Engineering/characterization contributions** (sensor noise density, sync offset ~2ms, accelerometer scale factor accuracy 0.03%, bias drift bound) are solid, narrowly-scoped empirical measurements useful for anyone replicating this on Insta360 hardware, but are specific to the X5/X6 models and (per their own caveat) one third-party camera unit.

## Caveats — stated and inferred

- **Small ground-truth sample size** (stated/inferred): Only 9 LiDAR surveys and 1 fiducial-marker survey constitute true external ground truth; the main accuracy headline numbers (0.81% vs 1.94% MAE) rest on just these 10 surveys. (inferred: this is a fairly small N for strong generalization claims)
- **No independent ground truth on the larger production sample** (stated): On the 50-survey "production set B" — the largest dataset — there is no LiDAR or other independent reference; the paper explicitly says the 0.3% discrepancy between IMU and smartphone scales "cannot be attributed to either reference" and that this is "left to future work." This means the paper's largest-scale validation is actually inconclusive by its own admission.
- **LiDAR reference itself not independently validated** (stated): the authors note the consumer LiDAR scanner's own drift over long paths "has not been characterised independently," and that a tape-measurement ground truth would be needed to fully validate either reference.
- **Camera/hardware generalization gap** (stated): all ground-truth-backed surveys were recorded with the X5; the X6 is only characterized as a sensor in isolation, not validated end-to-end on real surveys. Also, the static noise/bias characterization was done on a third, different camera unit than the ones used in the actual surveys, so stated as "orders of magnitude rather than unit-specific figures."
- **Unexplained lens discrepancy** (stated): choosing lens 0 vs lens 1 of the dual-fisheye rig shifts the mean scale ratio by ~0.6%, comparable in size to the main reported production-set bias, and "the origin of the difference between the two lenses has not been isolated" — a potentially confounding unexplained factor.
- **No non-linear refinement** (stated): the method stops at the closed-form linear solution and does not apply the non-linear/inertial-only refinement (e.g., Campos et al.) standard in the visual-inertial SLAM literature, which the authors note could further improve results — i.e., this may be a non-optimal version of the method, understating potential accuracy.
- **Root cause of the joint-solution bias not mechanistically isolated** (stated): the paper identifies that violated "constant gravity direction" assumptions are the likely cause of the 3.4% joint-solution bias but explicitly states "the specific mechanism that makes the effective gravity vary along a survey has not been isolated in this study" — the explanation is plausible but not confirmed.
- **Partial reproducibility gap for one dataset** (stated): results in Section 5.4 (production set A, 12 surveys) were computed with an older joint estimator and could not be fully recomputed with the improved segment-wise method because 9 of 12 reconstructions were no longer available — only 3 were re-verified.
- **Reliance on an internal, pipeline-specific SfM system** (inferred): All evaluations use one company's proprietary production COLMAP-family pipeline with specific feature/matching choices; results may not transfer to other SfM pipelines, camera rigs, or reconstruction quality regimes — not addressed in the paper as a generalization question.
- **Excluded/discarded data not fully accounted** (stated): four recordings were excluded from the study for various reasons (discontinuities, non-metric units, no reference available), and recordings from a previous pipeline generation couldn't be used at all due to stabilization decoupling image orientation from the gyroscope — suggesting real deployment constraints beyond the clean cases studied.
- **Correlation-based diagnosis treated as evidence without full attribution** (inferred): the claim that the production-set scale discrepancy "grows with the extent of the smartphone anchor" is based on a correlation (r≈0.5) that the authors admit is "carried largely by eight surveys" and "one of them... weighs heavily on the correlation" — a fairly fragile statistical pattern presented with appropriate hedging but still worth flagging as weak evidence.
