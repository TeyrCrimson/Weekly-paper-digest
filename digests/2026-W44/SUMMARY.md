# Weekly summary

## The week in one paragraph
Four papers this week split across two concerns: getting multi-component pipelines to compose correctly, and catching where multi-stage safety/trust boundaries leak. On composition, one paper shows an Insta360 camera's embedded IMU can replace a smartphone-based metric-scale anchor for SfM, but only once scale/gravity/bias are solved per short motion segment rather than jointly — evidence is solid against LiDAR ground truth but thin (no independent reference) on the largest production dataset. A smaller CV paper finds that chaining an independently-trained detector and segmentor for small-object tasks silently discards over half of otherwise-correct segmentations if you crop to detector boxes, though this is a preliminary, single-domain (71-object) result. On agent safety, one paper shows steganographic image payloads can bypass text-based prompt-injection defenses and proposes decode-time provenance binding as a fix, validated mainly via internal ablations on a small synthetic testbed. The other shows, via a live red-team competition, that a safety-judging LLM with a narrower input scope than the agent it polices creates an exploitable "scope gap," though the headline numbers are a cherry-picked best run, not a controlled success rate. The throughline: naive composition of independently-built components — sensors, models, or safety stages — introduces systematic errors or blind spots that only surface once you specifically test the seam.

## Themes
- **Composing independent pipeline components hides systematic errors at the seam** — *Metric Scale for Insta360 SfM* and *On Detection Reliability for Small Object Segmentation*. Both show that when two independently-run components (camera + IMU; detector + segmentor) are combined naively — sharing gravity/bias across a whole survey, or cropping segmentation to detector boxes — you get a systematic bias (3.4% scale overestimate) or a silent failure mode (>50% of correct segmentations fall outside predicted boxes). Both fixes are narrow and validated on small, single-domain samples (10 ground-truth surveys; 71 objects), so the "fix" is demonstrated but not shown to generalize.
- **Safety/trust boundaries in multi-stage agent pipelines are porous by construction** — *Agentic AI Defense: Decode-Time Provenance Binding* and *Scope-Gap Injection*. Both establish that when one pipeline stage (a decode tool, a safety-judge LLM) sees less than the stage that ultimately acts (the agent, the execution auditor), attackers can route malicious content through exactly that gap — image steganography invisible to text scanners, or attack specifics hidden in attachments the judge never reads. Together they establish the vulnerability class convincingly; only the first offers a mechanism (decode-time binding + independent taint ledger) with clean internal ablations, while the second only demonstrates the gap exists, on an uncontrolled, cherry-picked basis.

## Read these
- **Metric Scale for Insta360 Structure-from-Motion from the Camera's Embedded IMU** — the best-evidenced paper of the four (LiDAR + fiducial + public-benchmark validation, a real ablation isolating the actual fix) and the result is directly actionable for anyone running SfM+IMU pipelines.
- **Agentic AI Defense: Decode-Time Provenance Binding for MCP** — identifies a genuinely new blind spot in provenance-based agent defenses (trust labeled too late, at text-return rather than decode-time) and backs the fix with a four-component ablation showing each piece is independently necessary, not just an end-to-end attack demo.
- **Scope-Gap Injection** — a live red-team result against a real competition safety pipeline showing a safety judge can have a narrower input scope than the agent it's gating; the structural finding (mismatched channel coverage between pipeline stages) generalizes beyond this one competition even though the specific win numbers are a cherry-picked best run.

## Skip unless
- **On Detection Reliability for Small Object Segmentation** — skip unless you specifically chain a separate detector and segmentor and crop segmentation to detector boxes; the warning (box-gating can discard most correct masks) is real but only demonstrated on one small, single-domain dataset (71 objects, adenovirus TEM images).
