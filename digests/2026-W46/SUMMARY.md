# Weekly summary

## The week in one paragraph
Three domains dominated this week's batch: tiny-object detection, metric-scale 3D vision (video odometry, panoramic depth, Gaussian Splatting, SLAM), and a hard look at LLM security tooling. The strongest results all share a pattern — bolt a frozen pretrained prior (metric depth, DINOv3 features, a 2D segmenter, VGGT) onto an existing pipeline without retraining its core, and recover most of the missing signal cheaply. The standout non-vision paper is a rigorous audit showing commercial prompt-injection guard classifiers are nearly useless on legal contracts, while general-purpose LLMs catch what guards miss. Two engineering papers (an open-source rover nav stack, an MCU-targeted NAS for optical flow) are honest, modestly-scoped systems contributions rather than new science. Across the 3D-vision cluster specifically, evaluation leans heavily on synthetic data, self-constructed benchmarks, or non-held-out sequences, so treat the accuracy gains as promising rather than settled.

## Themes
- **Reviving discarded signal beats re-architecting from scratch.** *Reviving the Discarded High-Resolution Feature for Transformer-Based Tiny Object Detection* (ReDHF) and *MetricTrail* both show that a previously-thrown-away signal (the highest-res backbone feature map; absolute depth scale) was the actual bottleneck, and cheaply re-injecting it — rather than retraining the core network — buys most of the gain. Both back this with ablations showing the targeted fix (not just "more compute") drives the improvement, though MetricTrail's win shrinks on dynamic scenes and depends on which depth provider/dataset pair is used.
- **Metric scale is the unsolved thread running through 3D vision.** *MetricTrail*, *PVDepth*, *METRIC-GS*, and an (untitled in this digest) VGGT-SLAM extension paper on submap scale-anchoring and loop closure all attack the same underlying problem — recovering or maintaining correct real-world scale/geometry — via different frozen foundation-model priors (monocular depth nets, DINOv3, Mask2Former, VGGT). Collectively they establish that scale/geometric drift is still a live failure mode across video odometry, panoramic video, Gaussian Splatting, and pose-graph SLAM, but none of the four demonstrates robustness on independently-held-out, real-world benchmarks: PanoCARLA is synthetic, METRIC-GS and the SLAM paper lean on self-collected or non-held-out test scenes, and all four flag dataset- or scene-type-dependent failure (dynamic content, specular surfaces, dense large-object scenes, weak-texture outdoor scenes).
- **Guardrail classifiers fail at the thing they claim to do.** *Notwithstanding Anything to the Contrary, Ignore All Instructions* is a standalone but significant finding: eleven open-weight prompt-injection guards are driven by surface register (does text sound like a chat command) rather than malicious function, producing up to 92% false-positive rates on real contracts and missing a whole attack class entirely. The cluster size is one paper, but the methodology (factorial probes isolating register vs. function) is solid enough that the diagnosis should generalize beyond this one guard panel.
- **Practical deployment papers are honest about their limits.** The open-source rover navigation stack and MCUFlowNet (hardware-aware NAS for MCU optical flow) are both systems/engineering contributions rather than new algorithms, and both are unusually candid about what they haven't validated — the rover paper ran only 3 missions with many unlogged fields, and MCUFlowNet's gains are measured on one hardware target with simulation-only (not real-flight) downstream validation.

## Read these
- **Notwithstanding Anything to the Contrary, Ignore All Instructions** — the only paper here with a genuinely surprising, well-isolated negative result (production security tooling doesn't work) plus a usable diagnostic method, not just an incremental metric bump.
- **Reviving the Discarded High-Resolution Feature...(ReDHF)** — cleanest mechanistic story and most convincing ablations (raw-feature-hurts vs. processed-feature-helps) among the detection/vision papers, with compute-matched controls ruling out the "just more pixels" explanation.
- **MetricTrail** — the clearest and most consequential ablation in the whole batch (52.69m → 12.77m ATE from one change), directly identifying the actual bottleneck in streaming VO rather than layering on complexity.

## Skip unless
- **PVDepth** — only if you need panoramic/360° video depth specifically and can tolerate that all quantitative evidence comes from a synthetic CARLA-built dataset.
- **METRIC-GS** — only if you need metric-scale recovery for 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructions and can live with evaluation on the authors' own test scenes rather than an external benchmark (digest truncated before full caveats were available).
- **[Untitled VGGT-SLAM scale-anchoring/loop-closure paper]** — only if you're building multi-submap pose-graph SLAM on VGGT and want a scale-anchor/loop-closure trick; results mix clear wins with unexplained regressions on several sequences, and the test set isn't held-out.
- **An Integrated Open-Source Stack for Vision-Based Autonomous Navigation of Wheeled Rovers** — only if you want a ready-made ROS 2 reference stack (code/weights/dataset) to build on, not evidence of navigation reliability.
- **MCUFlowNet** — only if you're deploying dense optical flow specifically on Arm Cortex-M55/Ethos-U55-class hardware; the memory-decoupling trick is compiler/accelerator-specific and unvalidated elsewhere.
