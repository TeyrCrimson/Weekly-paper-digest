# PVDepth: Panoramic Video Depth Estimation via Geometry-Aware Spatiotemporal Adaptation

**Authors:** Chuanxin Song, Peixi Peng

**arXiv:** [2611.07873](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07873)

**Categories:** cs.CV

**Submitted:** 2026-11-05

**Relevance:** 8/10 — matched: Metric and temporally-consistent monocular depth

---

## TL;DR
PVDepth adapts a pretrained perspective video-depth diffusion model (DepthCrafter) to panoramic (equirectangular) video by fixing two ERP-specific problems: non-uniform spatial sampling density and non-linear motion near the poles. It does this via a noise-initialization scheme that anneals from planar to spherical noise, plus an auxiliary cubemap branch that performs temporal attention in a distortion-free representation and injects a correction back into the ERP branch. The authors also release PanoCARLA, a large synthetic panoramic RGB-D video dataset built in CARLA, since no such dataset previously existed at scale.

## First-principles breakdown

**The problem.** Depth estimation from monocular video means predicting a per-pixel distance map from RGB frames. For panoramic (360°) video, the image is stored as an Equirectangular Projection (ERP) — a spherical image unwrapped onto a flat rectangle, the same transform used for world maps. This mapping is not uniform: areas near the top/bottom (poles) get stretched enormously, so a small amount of real-world content near the poles occupies a huge number of pixels, while content near the "equator" is represented at roughly correct density. This creates two distinct issues:

1. **Spatial issue:** models trained on ordinary (perspective) photos assume pixels roughly uniformly sample the scene. Applied to ERP, they waste capacity on oversampled polar regions and may overfit to this distortion rather than learning real structure.
2. **Temporal issue:** when something moves smoothly in 3D space, its projected trajectory in ERP pixels is heavily warped near the poles — a straight, smooth path becomes jerky and non-linear in pixel space. Standard video-depth architectures model temporal consistency with 1D attention purely along the time axis per-pixel, implicitly assuming nearby frames show roughly linear pixel motion. That assumption breaks down specifically in the distorted polar regions.

**Why prior approaches fail.** Existing panoramic depth methods are mostly single-image (no temporal consistency → flicker across frames), or "predict-then-optimize" pipelines that run per-frame depth then post-hoc enforce consistency via geometric/SLAM-style optimization (e.g., ViPE, Matrix-3D). These are slow, assume mostly static scenes, and degrade with moving objects. Directly fine-tuning a perspective video-depth model on panoramic data (the "obvious" fix) still inherits the planar inductive bias and the pixel-domain temporal attention, so it doesn't fully close the gap — the paper shows fine-tuned DepthCrafter is a much stronger baseline than zero-shot, but PVDepth's additional mechanisms still improve on top of it. Also, there was previously no large-scale panoramic RGB-D *video* dataset to even train/evaluate end-to-end models — existing panoramic datasets are static images or indoor-only.

**PanoCARLA (the dataset fix).** Built in the CARLA driving simulator. Two-stage pipeline: (1) record realistic 6-DoF camera trajectories by actually driving a car with CARLA's autopilot (rather than hand-crafted waypoint interpolation, which produces unnatural motion), then (2) replay those recorded poses on a "ghost rig" — a virtual 6-camera RGB-D rig detached from any vehicle mesh — to get an occlusion-free 360° view, including simulated drone-like altitude variation (2–20m). Six perspective views per frame are stitched into ERP: RGB views are rendered with overlapping 120° FOV and feather-blended at seams; depth is converted from the simulator's planar Z-depth into true Euclidean (radial) distance before stitching, since naive Z-depth stitching is geometrically wrong across the spherical layout. Result: ~127K frames, 117 clips, >50km of travel, outdoor scenes only.

**Mechanism 1 — Progressive Sphere-aware Noise Initialization (PSNI).** Diffusion models generate/denoise an image starting from random Gaussian noise and iteratively refining it; the statistical properties of the initial noise matter because the model learned to denoise under assumptions from its training data. Standard video diffusion assumes i.i.d. uniform Gaussian noise, matched to the roughly uniform sampling of perspective images. For ERP, the paper instead samples noise on a 3D sphere and projects it down to the ERP grid (mimicking how the real image signal itself was formed), so the noise's spatial density pattern matches the real information density pattern of ERP (sparse/stretched at poles). But switching to this "spherical noise" abruptly would destabilize the pretrained model's priors, so they anneal: initial noise is a weighted mix `ε = w_s·ε_sphere + w_n·ε_normal`, where the normal-noise weight decays linearly over the first 5K training steps from 1 down to a small residual (0.1, kept permanently rather than going to zero) while the spherical weight grows correspondingly (constrained so total variance stays 1, i.e., it's a proper interpolation, not just an unweighted sum). This is a curriculum: early training leans on the robust pretrained planar behavior, later training leans toward the geometrically-correct spherical behavior, and a small residual of planar noise is kept because ablations showed it helps.

**Mechanism 2 — Cube-rectified Temporal Modeling (CRTM).** A cubemap is the classic alternative panoramic representation: the sphere is projected onto the 6 faces of a cube, each face being an ordinary undistorted perspective-like image. Motion that looks jerky/non-linear in ERP (especially near poles) looks smooth/linear in the cube faces, because cube faces don't have the latitude-dependent stretching. So CRTM adds an auxiliary branch: it reprojects the ERP feature maps into 6 cubemap faces, runs the (same, weight-shared) temporal attention layer on these cube features — now operating on data where the "nearby frames move roughly linearly" assumption actually holds — and reprojects the resulting rectified features back into ERP. This correction is added to the main ERP branch's output via a "zero-initialized" projection layer (a trick from ControlNet: the added layer starts at zero output so training begins identical to the unmodified baseline and gradually learns to blend in the correction, improving stability). Training is staged: Stage I fine-tunes spatial layers with PSNI (20K steps); Stage II freezes those and trains only the new CRTM projection layers (another 20K steps) — ablations show this two-stage, frozen-spatial approach beats joint one-stage training or leaving spatial layers trainable in stage 2.

## Key insights

- **Claimed and evidence-supported:** Both PSNI and CRTM independently improve over the fine-tuned-baseline (Table 3), and combining them is better than either alone — a real complementary spatial+temporal fix, not just one trick doing all the work. The ablations (Tables 3–6) are reasonably thorough: they isolate spherical-vs-annealed-vs-residual-noise choices, CRTM fusion/conditioning choices, and training-stage choices, and show that a naive "just train longer" baseline doesn't match the gains (ruling out the "components just mean more compute" objection).
- **The "sphere-aware noise matches sampling density" argument for diffusion models is a genuinely non-obvious idea** — reformulating noise initialization as a geometry-matching problem, rather than adding a new learned module, is architecturally clean (zero extra parameters claimed for PSNI).
- **Zero-shot generalization claims are more tentative:** qualitative results on Web360 and indoor scenes (no ground truth, SSIM-based reprojection proxy on GenEx) support "looks smoother/more consistent" but this is weaker evidence than the quantitative PanoCARLA benchmark, which is itself synthetic.
- **The dataset itself may be as useful a contribution as the method** — it directly targets a stated bottleneck (no public panoramic RGB-D video data), and its "ghost rig" trick for removing ego-vehicle self-occlusion while preserving realistic trajectories is a practical engineering contribution independent of the depth model.
- The efficiency comparison (Table 8) shows PVDepth is much faster than optimization-based competitors (ViPE, Matrix-3D) while being more accurate — a genuine practical advantage of the end-to-end approach, though it's slower than single-pass regression methods like UniK3D.

## Caveats — stated and inferred

- **Training and primary evaluation are entirely on synthetic data (PanoCARLA/CARLA).** `stated` (implicitly, by construction) — real-world evaluation is only qualitative (Web360, no depth GT) or via an indirect reprojection-SSIM proxy (GenEx Real-World), not direct depth accuracy. `inferred` as a significant generalization gap risk, though the authors do report this proxy evaluation as partial mitigation.
- **Outdoor-only training data; explicitly weaker indoor generalization and no quantitative indoor evaluation.** `stated` directly in the Limitations section ("our method... limitations under domain shifts... generalization of PVDepth to indoor settings may still be limited... do not report quantitative indoor evaluation due to the lack of publicly available annotated indoor panoramic video datasets").
- **Fails on specular/reflective surfaces (e.g., water).** `stated` directly, with the caveat that competing methods fail similarly, somewhat softening the criticism but confirming it's unresolved.
- **Higher inference latency/FLOPs than the baseline DepthCrafter and than regression-based methods (UniK3D).** `stated` directly in Table 8 and Limitations, attributed to the bidirectional ERP↔Cube projection overhead; "future work" is left to address it.
- **The real-world reprojection-based evaluation (Appendix F) has a circularity concern the authors themselves flag:** depth predictions are scale-aligned to ViPE's own depth output and evaluated using ViPE-estimated poses, which could bias results in PVDepth's favor if correlated. The authors partially address this with a second pose source (MapAnything) for robustness, but this is still an indirect metric (SSIM of reprojected RGB, not depth accuracy) over a small sample (50 clips). `stated` (authors raise and partially mitigate it), `inferred` residual risk since it remains an indirect proxy.
- **No public panoramic RGB-D video dataset exists for benchmarking, so all quantitative comparisons are against the authors' own newly-created dataset/split** — meaning there's no independent, pre-existing benchmark confirming the gains generalize beyond PanoCARLA's distribution (CARLA simulator visual style, Town01-10 environments). `inferred`.
- **Baselines for the "image-based" and "post-processing" categories are not all given equal fine-tuning opportunity** — most are evaluated zero-shot except DepthAnywhere; it's unclear whether other image-based or post-processing baselines would close the gap with comparable fine-tuning/compute, making some comparisons potentially favor PVDepth's own fine-tuned advantage rather than architecture alone. `inferred`.
- **CRTM's "weight-sharing" and zero-init design choices are justified qualitatively ("stability," "parameter efficiency") rather than via a deeper theoretical analysis** — the explanations for why specific ablation results occur (e.g., why residual planar noise helps) are post-hoc and speculative ("this implies...", "likely because..."). `inferred`.
- **Code/data availability is promised ("will be released") but not confirmed as released at submission time**, so independent verification of claims isn't currently possible from the paper alone. `inferred`.
