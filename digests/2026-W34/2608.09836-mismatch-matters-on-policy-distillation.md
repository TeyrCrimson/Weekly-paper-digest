# Mismatch Matters: On-Policy Distillation Beyond Token Agreement

**Authors:** Zichao Yu, Chengzhi Yu, Shengze Xu, Yujin Han, Bingqing Jiang, Xu Wang, Difan Zou

**arXiv:** [2608.09836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09836)

**Categories:** cs.AI, cs.CL

**Submitted:** 2026-08-10

**Relevance:** 9/10 — matched: Post-inference detection and repair of degenerate output

---

## TL;DR
On-policy distillation (OPD) for LLMs can silently fail: students learn to repeat text in loops that the teacher, conditioned on that same repetitive prefix, also finds highly probable — producing near-perfect teacher-student agreement despite globally garbage output ("degenerate agreement"). The paper introduces TIDE, which instead targets teacher-student *mismatch*, applying bounded suppression to tokens the student over-generates and analytically injecting probability mass for tokens the teacher prefers but the student rarely samples. On Qwen3 teacher-student pairs with strong mismatch, TIDE lifts Avg@8 from 6.9% to 20.3% while cutting response length 3.6x and nearly eliminating malformed outputs.

## First-principles breakdown

**Background: what is on-policy distillation?** Standard knowledge distillation trains a small "student" model to mimic a large "teacher" by minimizing KL divergence on fixed teacher-generated or human data. OPD instead lets the student generate its own rollouts (sequences of tokens), and at each token position asks the teacher "what would you have predicted here?" The student is pushed to match the teacher's probability at each of its own sampled tokens. This is implemented as a policy-gradient update where the "advantage" of a token is `a_t = log p_teacher(token) - log q_student(token)`: positive means teacher liked it more than student did (reinforce it), negative means the opposite (suppress it). This is on-policy because supervision only occurs at states the student itself visits, unlike SFT which trains on fixed teacher demonstrations regardless of what the student would actually produce (this mismatch between training and deployment distributions is "exposure bias").

**The failure mode: degenerate agreement.** The paper observes that during OPD training, students increasingly fall into repetitive loops (e.g., repeating "I hope it is correct" 149 times) — the fraction of rollouts with loops rises from 16.8% to 48% over training. Why does this happen and why doesn't the teacher correct it? Because the teacher is *conditioned on the student's own generated prefix*. If the student has already repeated a phrase 10 times, "predict the next token" becomes trivially easy — of course it's probably the same phrase again — so the teacher's distribution collapses toward agreeing with the student's continuation too. The paper verifies this directly: artificially repeating a phrase 16 times drops teacher-student KL divergence by 63x and teacher entropy along with it. This means the reverse-KL loss (which the OPD gradient approximates) reports "we agree, nothing to fix" exactly at the moments the response is falling apart. The student can effectively "hack" the teacher signal by making the teacher's own predictions parrot the degenerate continuation — a subtle exploit the authors call "student-induced teacher hacking."

**Why prior approaches (token-selection methods) don't fix this.** A common idea in recent OPD work is to select only "high-agreement" tokens as the most reliable supervision signal, since where teacher and student agree ought to indicate the student is doing well. But degenerate agreement is exactly high-agreement — repetition manufactures artificially high overlap. So overlap-based selection heuristics can't distinguish genuine competence from a repetition exploit.

**The paper's reframing: two types of mismatch, each broken differently.** Instead of chasing agreement, TIDE looks at where teacher and student *disagree*, and splits disagreement into two structurally different cases, rooted in an asymmetry inherent to reverse KL (the objective OPD implicitly minimizes): reverse KL punishes the student hard for putting probability on tokens the teacher dislikes, but is weak at pulling the student toward tokens the teacher likes that the student currently ignores.
- **Student-excess tokens**: tokens the student actually generates (so they're observable in rollouts) but which the teacher assigns near-zero probability. The correction term for these, `log(q_student/p_teacher)`, blows up unboundedly as teacher probability → 0. In practice, the single worst 1% of tokens contribute nearly half the total training gradient — a small number of extreme corrections can dominate and destabilize training.
- **Student-deficit tokens**: tokens the teacher strongly prefers but the student almost never samples (median student probability 2×10⁻⁴; 95.5% have <1% chance of appearing even across 4 rollouts). Because standard OPD only corrects tokens that actually get *sampled*, these valuable "the teacher would have said this instead" signals are essentially invisible to the training process — a structural coverage gap, not just bad luck.

**TIDE's two-part fix:**
1. *For excess tokens*: instead of using the raw unbounded log-ratio, TIDE applies a Hellinger-shaped transform `h(a) = 2[exp(a/2) − 1]`, which is mathematically bounded (stays within (-2,0) for negative advantages) yet behaves like the original correction for small mismatches (first-order equivalent near zero). The paper proves this transform is the exact gradient of squared Hellinger distance between teacher and student — so it's not an arbitrary clipping heuristic but corresponds to a legitimate alternative divergence measure. Only the top fraction (ρ⁻=0.2) of positions ranked by excess magnitude get this correction; the rest are left alone.
2. *For deficit tokens*: rather than waiting to sample the missing tokens (which may take thousands of rollouts), TIDE directly looks at the teacher's top-K candidate tokens at each position, computes a "deficit score" measuring how poorly the student's probability mass covers this teacher-preferred set (decomposed into misallocation within the top-K plus insufficient total mass on the set), and for the most deficient positions (top ρ⁺=0.2 fraction) directly trains the student via cross-entropy against the teacher's (renormalized) top-K distribution — no sampling required.

Positions with neither meaningful excess nor deficit ("matched" tokens) are dropped from the loss entirely, removing redundant/uninformative supervision.

## Key insights

- **Genuinely new diagnostic finding**: degenerate agreement / student-induced teacher hacking — the demonstration that a teacher conditioned on a student's degenerate rollout will itself collapse toward endorsing the degeneracy, making KL-based agreement metrics actively misleading. This is a mechanistic explanation for known "length inflation" problems in OPD, not just a new fix.
- **The excess/deficit asymmetry** is a clean, theoretically grounded (tied to reverse-KL's known zero-forcing/mode-seeking behavior) framework for why some mismatch is easy-but-dangerous and other mismatch is informative-but-invisible. Both a diagnostic contribution and the basis for the method.
- **Evidence supports**: consistent gains over OPD and several recent baselines (FiRe-OPD, PowerOPD, AOPD, GRPO) across two teacher-student pairs, with much larger gains under strong mismatch (13.4 pt Avg@8 improvement) than weak mismatch (~1 pt) — a plausible and internally consistent pattern (the mechanism matters more when the models disagree more). Ablations (Table 4/5) show the three components (selection, Hellinger shaping, deficit recovery) are individually helpful and complementary, and the deficit-only intervention alone raising Avg@8 from 6.87 to 18.32 is a fairly strong isolated demonstration that deficit tokens carry real signal, not just an artifact of the full method.
- **Claimed vs. supported**: the paper claims TIDE is broadly useful for "reliable on-policy distillation" generally; the evidence is specifically about math reasoning distillation between Qwen3 model pairs. The mechanism (repetition exploiting teacher conditioning) is plausible generally but is only demonstrated in this narrow setting.

## Caveats — stated and inferred

- **stated**: "TIDE assumes a locally reliable teacher" and the evaluation is limited to two teacher-student pairs and mathematical reasoning; dialogue, code, and multilingual settings are explicitly left open.
- **stated**: The theory is "fixed-state, operator-level" and "gives no global convergence guarantee"; adaptive keep-rate schedules are noted as unstudied.
- **stated**: The gate keep-rate ρ=0.2 was "fixed before benchmark evaluation" rather than tuned — presented as a strength for avoiding overfitting to test metrics, but this also means no evidence is given that 0.2 is actually optimal (sensitivity analysis in Table 8 shows some sensitivity, e.g. ρ=0.5 gives higher Avg@8 than the chosen default in one run).
- **inferred**: Only two teacher-student pairs and one training corpus (DAPO-Math-17K) are used; both are math-reasoning specific with rule-based, symbolic-equivalence grading — no natural-language, code-generation, or subjective-quality tasks are tested, despite the paper's general framing of OPD as a "core component of modern LLM post-training."
- **inferred**: Baselines (FiRe-OPD, PowerOPD, AOPD) are all very recent (2026 arXiv preprints, contemporaneous with this work) and reimplemented by the authors rather than run via official released code/checkpoints in most cases — reimplementation fidelity is asserted ("we verify parity") but not independently auditable from the text.
- **inferred**: All models are trained for exactly one epoch with a single fixed checkpoint (no checkpoint selection via validation), and hyperparameters like λ=1.0 and K=16 are chosen via limited sweeps (Table 5 shows only 4 values of λ tested) — the robustness of results to broader hyperparameter space is not established.
- **inferred**: The qualitative case studies (Section E) are explicitly selected via a fixed filter (OPD gets 0/8 correct, TIDE gets 8/8 correct) — while the authors are transparent that this is a "fixed rule rather than manual inspection" to avoid cherry-picking, this filter by construction selects for the paper's strongest contrast cases and is not representative of the full performance distribution.
- **inferred**: No wall-clock/compute-cost comparison is given for the extra forward pass over the teacher's top-K candidates required by the deficit branch (stated as "a cost comparable to top-K OPD" without quantification), so the added training cost of TIDE relative to plain OPD is not addressed in the paper in concrete terms.
- **inferred**: Evaluation uses a single rule-based symbolic verifier with no LLM-based grading cross-check, and Avg@8/Pass@8 are the only accuracy metrics reported — no analysis of statistical significance (confidence intervals) is given for the main accuracy tables, only for some diagnostic figures (e.g., Fig. 4a, Fig. 6a).
