# Calibrating the Uncertainty of Self-Supervised Monocular Depth without Labels

**Authors:** Jun Seok Kim, Rui Huang

**arXiv:** [2611.19188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19188)

**Categories:** cs.CV

**Submitted:** 2026-11-14

**Relevance:** 9/10 — matched: Metric and temporally-consistent monocular depth

---

## TL;DR
Self-supervised monocular depth networks output a per-pixel uncertainty σ, but the standard evaluation (ranking-based metrics like AUSE) never checks whether σ is the right *magnitude* — so a σ that's systematically half or twice the true error still scores perfectly. The authors show this miscalibration is universal (on KITTI, every method they test is off by 0.6–6×) and propose MonoCal, a two-part fix — a label-free scale constant from stereo pairs, plus retraining σ against disagreement with a second independently-trained network — that corrects both the average magnitude and its distribution across confidence/depth, without ever using ground-truth depth or more than one image at inference.

## First-principles breakdown

**The problem setup.** A monocular depth network predicts, from a single image, a depth value µ per pixel. Some networks also predict σ, meant to represent the standard deviation of the error on µ (i.e., "how wrong do you think you are here?"). This is useful for downstream systems (SLAM, mapping) that want to weight or discard uncertain depth readings — but only if σ's numeric scale is trustworthy, not just its relative ordering.

**Why prior evaluation is blind to this.** The common metric, AUSE (area under the sparsification error), works by progressively removing the pixels the model claims are least confident and watching whether error drops — i.e., it only tests whether σ *ranks* pixels correctly by error. It's invariant to monotonic rescaling: multiply every σ by 10, and AUSE doesn't change at all. So a network could report "σ=0.1m" when the real error is "2m" and look equally good as one that's perfectly calibrated. No published self-supervised uncertainty method had actually checked the real-world size of σ against error.

**Measuring the problem (scale vs. shape).** The paper defines a ratio r = error / σ. If σ is calibrated, r should be about 1 everywhere.
- *Scale*: is r ≈ 1 on average over all pixels? (A single global miscalibration factor.)
- *Shape*: does r stay ≈1 consistently — across different σ bins ("consistency": is small-σ as accurate relatively as large-σ?) and across different depth ranges ("adaptivity": is near-pixel σ as accurate as far-pixel σ?). They quantify shape error with ENCE/SI-ENCE metrics (binned deviation of r from 1, with SI-ENCE additionally removing the best-possible single scale correction, isolating shape issues a constant literally cannot fix).

They find on KITTI that MonoProb's (a leading self-supervised uncertainty method) error is nearly 2× its predicted σ (so scale is off), and even after fixing the average, the *shape* is still wrong: near pixels are 3× underestimated while middle-range pixels become overestimated once you apply one single correction factor — because a single multiplicative constant can only shift a log-log reliability curve sideways, not change its slope or straighten its bends.

**Why ground truth isn't a good fix either.** The "obvious" solution — fit the calibration constant against LiDAR ground truth — doesn't generalize: the correct constant differs across networks and datasets, so you'd need labels everywhere you deploy, defeating the purpose of self-supervised (label-free) depth.

**MonoCal's mechanism — two components:**
1. **Scale calibration (no retraining, no labels).** Instead of comparing µ to LiDAR, compare it to a depth estimate obtained independently from the *same* stereo pair via classical stereo matching (semi-global matching, SGBM) — a geometric, non-learned proxy depth, not ground truth. The residual between µ and this stereo depth stands in for the true error. Taking the median ratio of this residual to σ over a validation set yields a single scale constant s; multiplying σ by s realigns the average magnitude. This is remarkably close to what you'd get from real ground truth (within ×1.08 for 16/17 cases tested), yet needs no retraining and only needs a stereo pair during calibration — not at inference.
2. **Disagreement target (requires retraining σ only).** A constant can't fix shape (it only translates the log-log calibration curve). To fix shape, they retrain σ to predict the *disagreement* between the network's own depth and the depth predicted by a second, architecturally different self-supervised network (MonoViT). Two different networks tend to disagree exactly where at least one of them is wrong, so this disagreement acts as a free, label-free proxy signal for "where is the error large" — borrowing the classic ML idea that model disagreement correlates with predictive error (ensembles, Krogh & Vedelsby). Crucially, the gradient from this new loss term is blocked from touching µ (stop-gradient) — only σ is trained on it, so depth accuracy is preserved while σ gets reshaped. After this retraining, scale calibration (step 1) is applied on top to fix the remaining average-magnitude offset, since the disagreement signal itself isn't the right magnitude (two self-supervised networks tend to agree with each other more than either agrees with real depth).

The combination — retrain shape via disagreement, then rescale via stereo — is "MonoCal."

## Key insights

- **Genuinely new**: identifying that scale and shape are two *separable* calibration failures, and that the whole self-supervised depth literature has only ever measured ranking (not magnitude), so this miscalibration went undetected. This diagnostic framing (plus the SI-ENCE metric, an oracle-rescaled ENCE that isolates pure shape error) is a solid conceptual contribution independent of the fix.
- **Scale calibration from stereo residuals substituting for ground truth** is the paper's most practically useful and well-evidenced result: the label-free constant matches the ground-truth-fitted constant within ×1.08 for 16/17 estimates on KITTI — strong, concrete evidence it's a viable drop-in replacement for labeled calibration, with zero retraining cost.
- **Disagreement-based shape correction** is a plausible and reasonably well-supported idea (reduces consistency/adaptivity errors ~4× versus any constant, including ground-truth constant, on the main KITTI/MonoProb setup; generalizes to 2 of 3 other backbones and to two other datasets when only the constant is re-measured). However, the claimed universality is softer: on MonoViT, shape errors were already low and the method helped only marginally (consistency, not adaptivity); and the ablation shows a much cheaper alternative (self-distillation + per-bin constants, no disagreement target) is "comparable to MonoCal on KITTI," though it doesn't generalize as well to other datasets. So the disagreement target's advantage over simpler alternatives is real but more modest than the headline KITTI numbers suggest.
- **AUSE/ARU/RMSU are shown to be actively misleading**: a calibrated σ actually scores *worse* on ARU/RMSU than an overconfident one, because those pointwise metrics are minimized by underestimated variance (consistent with known failure modes of heteroscedastic regression losses). This is a useful methodological warning independent of MonoCal itself.

## Caveats — stated and inferred

- **Requires stereo pairs to measure the constant (stated).** Scale calibration is "label-free" but not sensor-free — it needs a stereo pair at calibration time. On nuScenes, which has no stereo pairs, they could only reuse KITTI's constant, yielding a scale off by 1.4×, undermining the "works anywhere without labels" claim.
- **Shape correction (disagreement target) is only trained/validated on KITTI (stated).** On Argoverse 1 and DrivingStereo, they only re-measure the scale constant on the KITTI-trained σ, rather than retraining the disagreement target natively on those datasets — so true cross-dataset generalization of the shape-fixing mechanism itself is untested.
- **Stereo-matching proxy has its own errors (stated).** SGBM stereo depth is explicitly noted to be noisier on DrivingStereo ("three times as many errors... as on KITTI"), and the paper acknowledges the median-ratio correction only works if the stereo residual's median matches the true error's median — an assumption that "is an empirical question," not a guarantee.
- **Single dataset for the core, strongest results (inferred).** The headline numbers (Fig. 1a, Table 2, the ×1.08 agreement claim) are all on KITTI; cross-dataset numbers (Argoverse1, DrivingStereo, nuScenes) are consistently weaker or partially untestable, suggesting the core claims are KITTI-specific to a meaningful degree.
- **Cherry-pick risk in backbone selection (inferred).** The disagreement target helps less (or barely) on MonoViT ("shape errors already low... only consistency decreases") — a case where the method's benefit is marginal is included, which is good for transparency, but means the "generalizes to other backbones" claim is weaker than the MonoProb results imply.
- **Comparable cheaper alternative exists (stated in ablation, likely under-emphasized in headline claims).** Self-distillation + per-(σ,depth)-bin constants (no second network, no new loss term) is "comparable to MonoCal on KITTI" — meaning part of MonoCal's benefit on its primary benchmark could be attributed to simpler post-hoc binning rather than the disagreement-target mechanism specifically; the paper's defense is that this cheaper alternative generalizes worse off-KITTI, but that is a secondary, less emphasized result.
- **AUSE slightly worse than the self-distilled MonoProb baseline (stated).** MonoCal's AUSE is "12% higher than that of the self-distilled MonoProb checkpoint," a result reported honestly but not emphasized in the abstract/intro framing.
- **Compute cost and reliance on a second trained network (stated).** Training requires ~3.7 hours on an RTX 2080 Ti plus access to a second, independently-trained self-supervised network of different architecture — a non-trivial dependency not needed by simpler constant-only baselines.
- **No code/artifact available yet for independent verification (inferred).** The abstract says "code will be made publicly available," meaning results in this version are not yet independently reproducible.
- **"Not addressed in the paper":** no discussion of computational/latency cost at inference (likely negligible since inference uses one image and unchanged µ, but not explicitly quantified), and no exploration of whether the disagreement-target approach would hold for outdoor scenes radically different from KITTI/Argoverse/DrivingStreet's driving domain (e.g., indoor, aerial).
