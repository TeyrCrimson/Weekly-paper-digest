# Weekly summary

## The week in one paragraph
Eight digests cluster around two unsolved problems — tiny/distant-target detection for maritime search-and-rescue, and metric scale in monocular depth — plus one standalone finding on multimodal LLM security. On SAR, a new tiny-object dataset, a simulated event-camera comparison, and a benchmark-leakage audit all point the same way: detectors still fail at small, distant, or still targets, and much of the field's reported progress rests on synthetic substitutes or leaky evaluation sets rather than verified real-world capability. On depth, the strongest methodological result of the week is a diagnostic showing that averaged accuracy scores hide near-ubiquitous scale failures, while Depth Pro 2, a self-supervised uncertainty fix, and a VIO-depth fusion system each make real but narrower, caveated progress on sharpness, calibration, and mapping respectively. Separately, a security paper shows that rendering documents as images blocks simple prompt-injection attacks but not targeted answer-replacement, and that even that partial protection collapses under a small attack search. Across both main clusters, the recurring caveat is the same: impressive headline numbers sit on simulation-only data, self-designed evaluation protocols, or small/non-independent test sets, so read the strong claims as directional, not settled.

## Themes

- **Tiny/distant-target detection for maritime SAR is still unsolved, and much of the evidence is synthetic or leaky.** *TinyWOD*, *Event-Based Person Detection for Maritime UAV Search*, and the untitled benchmark-leakage/cue-gating paper (HIT-UAV/HERIDAL audit) collectively show that detectors remain weak on 3–15px targets (AP75 and AR100 stay low in TinyWOD), that an event-camera's blur-robustness advantage only shows up on simulated events and synthetic exposure blur, and that two widely-used SAR benchmarks have been overstating recall via data leakage — with still/camouflaged people detected only 0–7.5% of the time in real field footage. Together: not a solved problem, and the field's own measuring sticks need repair before progress claims can be trusted.
- **Monocular depth's open problem is scale, not shape — and new architectures chip at it only partially.** *What the Averages Hide* shows 7.7–44% outright failure rates hide behind averaged metrics, with 98.3% of failures being pure scale (not structural) error — a diagnostic result with no proposed fix. *Depth Pro 2* and *Calibrating the Uncertainty of Self-Supervised Monocular Depth* each attack adjacent pieces (sharpness+temporal consistency; uncertainty-magnitude calibration) with real evidence but entangled ablations and synthetic-heavy validation. *Accord* fuses VIO and a depth foundation model to remove residual per-image scale bias in dense mapping, winning on 4 of 6 platforms but still capped by VIO drift and GPU-class compute. The cluster establishes that scale is the field's persistent bottleneck, and that current fixes are real but local, not general solutions.
- **Rendering as a prompt-injection defense is conditional, not categorical.** The *Text to Pixels* paper is a singleton but cuts against a prior "rendering works" narrative: image-rendering blocks simple append/reformat injections but a fixed-phrase answer-replacement attack still succeeds 77.96% of the time on one model, and an 8-candidate search closes most of the protection gap for two of three tested models. Establishes that published aggregate ASR numbers were masking which attacks actually survive.

## Read these
- **Depth Pro 2** — the only paper this week shipping a model that moves a documented three-way trade-off (sharpness vs. temporal consistency vs. metric accuracy) rather than just measuring or patching one axis; broadest practical impact, though its ablations don't fully isolate why it works.
- **What the Averages Hide** — reframes how every other depth paper this week (and in the literature) should be read: it's the diagnostic that explains *why* scale keeps reappearing as the residual problem everywhere else, backed by 79,308 image×model evaluations rather than a handful of benchmarks.
- **What Survives the Shift from Text to Pixels** — the week's only security paper, and it overturns a simple "rendering defeats prompt injection" takeaway with mechanistic evidence (text commands are locally encoded, image commands are smeared across visual tokens) that matters directly for anyone deploying image-rendered RAG/document QA.

## Skip unless
- **TinyWOD** — skip unless you're training or benchmarking tiny-target detectors for high-altitude maritime SAR and need pixel-scale-matched data; the whole dataset rests on an untested physical-equivalence assumption (shrunk target ≈ real target at altitude).
- **Event-Based Person Detection for Maritime UAV Search** — skip unless you're specifically evaluating event cameras for UAV SAR under motion blur; every result (events, exposure blur) is simulated from ordinary video, not captured by real event-camera hardware.
- **The untitled SAR benchmark-leakage / cue-gating paper** — skip unless you currently rely on HIT-UAV or HERIDAL reported numbers, or are designing an edge-ground verification cascade for a search drone; field validation is one site, two afternoons, a handful of people.
- **Calibrating the Uncertainty of Self-Supervised Monocular Depth without Labels** — skip unless your pipeline actually consumes the per-pixel σ from a self-supervised depth network (e.g., for SLAM weighting) rather than just the depth itself.
- **Accord** — skip unless you need real-time dense metric mapping from one camera + IMU on GPU-class compute; it inherits all VIO trajectory failures unfixed, and its "universal device" claim is only partially supported (wins on one phone, loses on another).
