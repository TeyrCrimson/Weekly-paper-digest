# Depth Pro 2: Sharp and Temporally Consistent Metric Monocular Depth

**Authors:** Peiyun Hu, Marko Mihajlovic, Lyndon Duong, Lahav Lipson, Wei Dong, Amaël Delaunoy, Stephan R. Richter, Vladlen Koltun

**arXiv:** [2611.26603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26603)

**Categories:** cs.CV, cs.LG

**Submitted:** 2026-11-19

**Relevance:** 10/10 — matched: Metric and temporally-consistent monocular depth

---

## TL;DR
Depth Pro 2 is a single transformer model from Apple that estimates dense, metric depth for both images and video at native resolution, resolving a long-standing trade-off where single-frame methods give sharp but temporally jittery depth and multi-frame methods give stable but blurry depth. It does this by jointly processing 1–32 frames with alternating within-frame and cross-frame attention on a DINOv3 backbone, trained with a multi-view consistency loss, and claims state-of-the-art results simultaneously on metric accuracy, boundary sharpness, and temporal consistency across a four-model family (24M–872M params).

## First-principles breakdown

**The problem.** Monocular depth estimation infers a per-pixel distance map from an ordinary 2D image or video, without stereo or LiDAR. Two properties matter for downstream uses (e.g., synthetic background blur/bokeh): *boundary sharpness* (does the depth map respect fine object edges like fur, rather than bleeding across them) and *temporal consistency* (does a static object stay at the same predicted depth across consecutive video frames, rather than flickering). The paper observes these two properties are empirically anti-correlated across prior methods (Fig. 2): single-image models (e.g., InfiniDepth) produce sharp edges because they process each frame independently at high resolution, but have no mechanism to agree with themselves frame-to-frame, so predictions drift. Multi-frame/video models (e.g., π3, VGGT) enforce consistency via heavy mechanisms — long temporal attention windows, video diffusion priors, or joint pose/geometry optimization — but these are computationally expensive, forcing them to downsample input resolution, which smooths out fine boundaries.

**Why prior fixes fail.** The paper argues the consistency mechanisms themselves (long-context attention, diffusion denoising, multi-stage training curricula) are what force the resolution trade-off: more temporal context or diffusion sampling costs more compute, so to stay tractable these models shrink the input, losing detail.

**The mechanism.** Depth Pro 2's core idea is architectural simplicity rather than heavier machinery:
1. **Backbone**: Start from a DINOv3 Vision Transformer (pretrained for single images, chosen because DINOv3 produces high-resolution dense features).
2. **Flexible multi-frame attention**: Take the normal single-image self-attention blocks and, in the *last 2/3* of the network's layers, alternate between "attend only within this frame" and "attend across all frames in the clip." The first third of layers stays single-frame (self-attention only) because early layers mostly encode low-level texture, not cross-view correspondence — so there's no need to pay the cross-frame compute cost there. Crucially, no new parameters are added for this — they reuse existing attention weights, and they drop positional/frame-order encoding so the model is permutation-equivariant (order of frames doesn't matter, enabling arbitrary clip lengths from 1 to 32 at train time and unlimited at inference).
3. **Native resolution**: Unlike methods that resize/tile, images are split into 16×16 patches and processed at full native resolution with 2D rotary position embeddings (normalized by the longer image side to preserve aspect ratio info).
4. **Decoder**: A standard DPT (Dense Prediction Transformer) decoder fuses features from several intermediate transformer layers at multiple resolutions into one inverse-depth map per frame, using transposed convolutions instead of bilinear upsampling for sharper output.
5. **Metric depth via focal length**: The network predicts *inverse depth normalized by focal length* (not absolute distance). To get actual metric depth, you multiply by the true focal length (from EXIF) or, if unavailable, a dedicated lightweight estimator (a fine-tuned DINOv3-S model, 23.5M params) predicts the field of view, from which focal length follows geometrically. This decouples "what shape is the scene" from "what is the camera's zoom," a trick inherited from the original Depth Pro.
6. **Training objective**: Four loss terms summed: (a) a trimmed L1 on metric-scaled inverse depth (anchors absolute scale, synthetic data only, since real-world ground truth is noisier); (b) a scale/shift-normalized trimmed L1 applied to all data (handles datasets without known metric scale); (c) a multi-scale gradient-matching loss on synthetic data (sharpens boundaries by matching depth-map gradients at multiple pyramid scales); (d) a new **multi-view consistency loss** — using ground-truth camera poses (not noisy optical flow, as prior work used), project a pixel from frame i into frame j via its predicted depth, and penalize the gap between the reprojected depth and frame j's own predicted depth at that location. Pixels where this reprojection disagrees with ground-truth depth by >2% are excluded (these are occlusions/moving objects violating the static-scene assumption), so the loss still applies safely to dynamic video.
7. **Inference modes**: "Buffered streaming" processes overlapping fixed-length clips and blends predictions in the overlap region for smoothness; "unbuffered streaming" processes frames one at a time with causal (only-look-backward) cross-attention over a sliding window of cached keys/values, minimizing latency for live applications.

## Key insights

- **Genuinely new**: The reframing of "sharp vs. consistent" as a resolution/compute trade-off induced by prior architectural choices (rather than an intrinsic limit), and the demonstration that a single lightweight architectural tweak (partial cross-frame attention, no new parameters, native resolution) can escape it — this is the paper's central claimed contribution, and Figure 2/Tables 2–5 show empirical support: Depth Pro 2-H+ achieves the best or near-best average rank across sharpness (Table 4), temporal consistency (Table 5), and metric accuracy (Tables 2–3) simultaneously, where every prior method was strong on at most one axis.
- **Multi-view consistency loss using GT poses instead of optical flow**: A plausible, practically motivated design choice (more robust supervision signal than flow), though the paper doesn't isolate how much of the consistency gain comes from this loss term vs. the architecture itself (see caveats).
- **Lightweight, accurate focal-length estimator**: δ25 of 88.6% vs. 71.7% for Depth Pro's estimator, at a much smaller footprint (23.5M params) — a clear, well-isolated improvement with direct ablations (Table 23, Figure 11) supporting the design choices (separate estimator vs. bespoke head per model; ViT-S backbone; 512px training resolution).
- **Streaming inference engineering**: the buffered/unbuffered design with causal KV caching is a real systems contribution enabling practical deployment (53fps at 1080p for the smallest model), validated with concrete throughput/latency/memory measurements (Figure 10, 12, Table 22) — this is solid engineering evidence, not just a claim.
- **What's claimed vs. evidenced**: The "new Pareto frontier" claim is well supported by the benchmarks shown. However, the claim that this architecture is a general solution (vs. one well-tuned instance within a space Apple explored) rests on ablations the paper reports only for resolution, clip length, and streaming parameters — not on a controlled comparison isolating the contribution of cross-frame attention placement vs. the consistency loss vs. training data mix vs. native-resolution training (these are entangled; see caveats).

## Caveats — stated and inferred

- **Zero-shot evaluation protocol differences from prior published numbers** (stated): the authors explicitly note their own re-evaluated baseline scores differ from each baseline's published results because of differences in intrinsics handling, alignment, frame sampling, and resolution across studies — meaning absolute comparisons across papers aren't apples-to-apples, and the authors chose their own (reasonable but self-designed) protocol.
- **No Kinect-based datasets** (stated): excluded "due to noisy, biased, and incomplete ground truth" — a defensible but unilateral exclusion that removes some real-world low-cost-sensor scenarios from evaluation.
- **Metric depth accuracy still shows weaknesses on some datasets** (inferred): e.g. in Table 2, Depth Pro 2-H+ is not the top performer on Booster (65.7/43.1 vs. DAGE's 76.4/74.8) or on some δ1 columns — the paper emphasizes "average rank" as the headline metric, which can mask per-dataset underperformance; average-rank aggregation is itself a methodological choice that favors consistent-but-not-always-best performers.
- **Evaluation datasets and training datasets overlap risk** (inferred): the paper states results are "zero-shot" and gray out non-zero-shot entries for baselines, but doesn't fully disclose the exact dataset overlap checks for Depth Pro 2 itself across all 19 training datasets vs. all evaluation sets — this is asserted rather than independently audited in the text provided.
- **Ablation entanglement** (inferred): the core sharpness/consistency gains are attributed to several simultaneous changes — native-resolution training, restructured cross-frame attention placement (last 2/3 layers only), the multi-view consistency loss, and a different training-data/sampling mixture. The provided text does not show a controlled ablation isolating each factor's individual contribution to the main sharpness-vs-consistency result (only resolution and clip-length ablations are reported, in Table 21 and Appendix E.3) — so it's hard to know how much of the improvement is the attention architecture vs. the loss vs. training recipe.
- **Runtime/memory benchmarking is vendor-specific and self-reported** (inferred): all throughput/latency/memory figures (Figures 10, 12) are measured on Apple's own B200 setup under specific PyTorch eager-mode conditions without torch.compile; results may not generalize to other hardware/deployment stacks, and no independent verification is possible from the text.
- **Largest model is still slow** (stated implicitly in results): Depth Pro 2-H+, the model used for all the headline SOTA comparisons (Tables 2–6), runs at only 8 fps at 1080p — real-time use requires the smaller, less accurate S/B/L variants, meaning the "best of all three axes" claim is strongest only for the compute-heavy variant.
- **Synthetic-data reliance for sharpness-related losses** (stated): the multi-scale gradient loss and the trimmed-L1 metric loss are both restricted to synthetic data because real-world ground truth is considered too noisy — meaning the sharpness-boosting supervision is itself dependent on synthetic dataset realism/diversity, a known general risk for sim-to-real transfer that the paper does not separately stress-test here.
- **Jitter/temporal-consistency metric limitations** (stated): the authors note in Appendix B.3 (referenced but not included in the provided text) a discussion of "limitations" of their jitter metric, and report it is computed only on synthetic datasets with available dense ground-truth optical flow/point tracks (Sintel, Spring) — so temporal-consistency claims rest on a narrow, synthetic-heavy evaluation set rather than diverse real-world video.
- **No discussion of failure cases or negative results** (inferred): the paper presents comprehensive benchmark tables but the main text provided includes no qualitative or quantitative discussion of where Depth Pro 2 fails (e.g., fast motion, strong occlusion, extreme lighting) — "not addressed in the paper" based on the text available.
